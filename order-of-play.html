Order of Play

© Johnathan L. Bingham & Ostensible Cat Design

Quick Reference

Play is a loop of four-hour Watches. In each Watch you pick something to do, the Danger Die decides whether the forest interrupts you, you deal with whatever turns up, and then you tidy up before the next Watch. Challenges and Ordeals slot into the same loop when their Omens arrive.

Each session

  • Start: your Boon Points equal your Doom.
  • End: make a Doom save if you are Tested; unspent Boon is lost.

Each Watch

  1. Look around. Note the terrain, roll the weather, and check whether you are inside an Ordeal’s footprint.
  2. Choose what to do. Travel, Explore, Forage, Rest, Repair, Craft, a Ritual, or an Ordeal task.
  3. Roll the Danger Die when your action calls for it.
  4. Deal with the result. A quiet Still Watch, a Special Event, or an encounter.
  5. Encounter? Check surprise, roll disposition, then Parley, Hide, Flee, Ruse, fight, or Submit.
  6. Clean up. Wounds, Conditions, loot, Fragments, Doom.
  7. End the Watch. Burn light, eat and drink after rest, roll for Omens, count down Ruin dice, check Doom.

When an Omen arrives, the Challenge or Ordeal begins: travel to its sites, get through their encounters, make the task checks, and face the Cause before the Ruin die runs out.

The Game Loop - Order of Play

Play moves through one repeating sequence:

Start of Session → Strategic Phase → Encounter Trigger → Tactical Phase → Encounter Aftermath → Return to Strategic Phase → End of Session.

Challenges and Ordeals interrupt this sequence when their Omens arrive, then return play to the appropriate point in the loop. On the Fated Road, an Ordeal is played out inside this same loop: its sites are reached by travel, its tasks are guarded by encounters, and the Watches spent on it are what its Ruin die counts down.

Start of Session: Set Boon Points from current Doom, and review the Fated Goal, Prepared events and any running Ruin die.

Strategic Phase: Resolve travel, exploration, foraging, rest, repairs, crafting, rituals, Ordeal tasks, and resource management in Ticks, Marks, and Watches. Terrain, weather, and the current action determine whether the Danger or Dread Die is rolled.

Tactical Phase: Resolve immediate interaction through approach, conversation, deception, concealment, flight, pursuit, combat, or submission. Use rounds and precise positioning only when timing or location matters.

End of Session: Make any Doom save your Doom state calls for, and let unspent Boon Points lapse.

The order of play will vary slightly depending on the version of the Bleak Forest Game you are playing. For instance, in Escape the Bleak Forest, you begin at a hovel deep in the Bleak Forest, while in Bleak Forest: Hail Three Wyrd Sisters, you begin at a specified position on the map. Escape the Bleak Forest does not use Doom, Boon or the Fated Road; skip those steps when playing it. If you are using your own map and/or rules, follow the guidelines below to develop your own order of play. The order of play can be broken down into the Strategic and Tactical play phases as follows.

  1. Start the session. Set Boon Points equal to current Doom, and note any Prepared Challenge or Ordeal, any running Ruin die, and the Still Watch count.
  2. Frame the Strategic Phase. Determine terrain, weather, position, elapsed time, and current resources. Note whether the party is inside an active Ordeal’s footprint.
  3. Declare the strategic action. Travel, Explore, Forage, Rest, Repair, Craft, perform a Ritual or Sigil, or work an Ordeal task.
  4. Check for interruption. Roll the Danger or Dread Die when required. Resolve a Still Watch, Special Event, Omen, Manifestation, Setback, or other result before continuing the action.
  5. Enter the Tactical Phase if an encounter occurs. Identify the encounter, establish approach distance and visibility, determine surprise and disposition, then choose an approach. Roll initiative only when immediate action order matters.
  6. Resolve the encounter. Use Parley, Hide, Flee, Ruse, Combat, Submit, pursuit, or the encounter’s specific procedure.
  7. Apply aftermath. Record final position, elapsed time, damage, Conditions, Fatigue, expended resources, Durability, loot, Fragments, Doom, Identity Mark effects, and scenario consequences.
  8. Return to Strategic play. Resume the interrupted action if time remains and it is still possible; otherwise complete the End of Watch procedure and begin the next Watch.
  9. End the session. Resolve the End of Session steps.

Start of Session

Before the first Watch of each session, check the campaign’s standing:

  • Boon. Each character’s Boon Points equal their current Doom. Boon is set only now: Doom lost or regained during the session does not change it. During the session a character can earn up to 2 more by accepting trouble.
  • Doom state. Note each character’s Doom state. Pressed (Doom 4–6) makes triggering a Complication cost 1 Boon Point unless the character accepts it; Failing (Doom 1–3) puts Doom saves at Minor Disadvantage and counts the character as At Risk for the Danger Die.
  • The Fated Goal. Review the Goal, the band each character stands in (Bands and Gates), Threads held, and any Holds the Adversary has gained.
  • Events in progress. List each Challenge or Ordeal and its Fragments found, whether it is Prepared, how many Watches it has waited for its Omen, and the current value of any Ruin die.
  • Still Watch count. Carry over the number of consecutive Still Watches.

Strategic Order of Play

The Strategic Phase is the default state of play. Resolve each Watch in the order below. If an encounter, Challenge, or Ordeal begins, suspend the current strategic action and return to it only after the interrupting procedure is complete.

  1. Determine the terrain and position. Apply effects on movement, foraging, visibility, cover, and the Danger or Dread pool. Note whether the party is inside an active Ordeal’s footprint.
  2. Roll and apply weather. Weather lasts for the Watch and modifies movement, visibility, foraging, and encounter risk. Inside a footprint it also shapes Ordeal tasks done outdoors (Weather on the day).
  3. Declare the Watch action. Choose Travel, Explore, Forage, Rest, Repair, Craft, Ritual/Sigil, or an Ordeal Task (see the Strategic Action Reference).
  4. Roll the Danger or Dread Die when required. Escape the Bleak Forest normally uses the Danger Die when entering a new Exploration sub-hex. Hail Three Wyrd Sisters uses or supplements it with the Dread Die according to its regional rules. While an Ordeal is Prepared or under way, add 1 die inside its footprint for the Trouble (+1 more in the hex of a Major Ordeal’s Cause), to the usual 4d6 maximum (The Danger Die inside the footprint).
  5. Resolve the result.
    • No encounter (2–5): roll on the Still Watch table, +1 for each consecutive Still Watch, in strategic time.
    • Encounter (6+): roll on the Encounter Tables and begin the Tactical Phase. Inside a footprint, roll 1d6 first: on 4–6 the encounter belongs to the Ordeal and uses its encounter table.
    • Wyrd die shows 1: a Special Event of the pool’s tier. Inside a footprint, roll 1d6 instead: 1–2 a Setback strikes; 3–4 you meet a Witness or find a Fragment for another event; 5–6 the Trouble flares and you roll for the Omen now.
    • Omen: may trigger a Prepared Challenge or Ordeal.
  6. Complete the declared action. If an interrupting result does not prevent it, finish the action using the relevant travel, exploration, foraging, rest, crafting, ritual, or Ordeal rules.
  7. End the Watch. Work through the End of Watch Checklist, then begin the next Watch.

Strategic Action Reference

  • Travel: move into a new area and do the following:
    • Apply terrain and weather; record time and distance. A standard travel day is two Watches of movement; a third is a Forced March.
    • If a new area Hex, then return to Step 1.
    • If a sub-hex, return to Step 4 (Danger/Dread Die).
    • Travelling between an Ordeal’s sites is travel as normal; in a Major Ordeal each site is its own Overland hex.
  • Explore: Spend the Watch examining the current area in depth. To search beyond the road for a landmark, an encounter site, or a way through a hazard, move in 2 km Operational Hexes and roll the Danger Die for each new sub-hex. Make a Wits check (Difficult 16) to gain a Fragment for a Challenge or Ordeal. Inside a footprint, every Fragment of that Ordeal lies within it, each task’s Fragment at or on the way to that task’s site (Fragments, Omens and Threads); beware False Fragments.
  • Forage — attempt to find food, water, Healing Herbs, or Alchemical Components. Each attempt takes 1 Watch. Declare Herb Search or Component Search before rolling. Make a Wits check vs. the terrain’s Forage BAM!, then roll the Forage Die pool (1d6 base; +1d6 per Survival Focus rank, maximum 4d6 total). Roll all dice in the pool and take the highest result. Each Survival Focus rank also grants one additional Forage attempt per Watch — roll the full pool again for each additional attempt. Read the result on the Foraging table: Common results use Forage I, Uncommon Forage II, Rare Forage III, and Exceptional Forage IV. Nothing can be foraged for a task in dead forest.
  • Rest: Choose the type of rest you need (see Rest and Recovery):
    • Recovery (1 Tick): restores 1 Vitality and clears 1 Mishap die.
    • Short Rest (1 Watch): restores all Vitality, clears all Mishap dice and removes 1 Fatigue.
    • Full Rest (2 Watches): restores all Vitality, clears all Mishap dice and all Fatigue, and heals 1 point of each damaged attribute. A Full Rest in a safe place, outside any active Ordeal’s footprint, also restores 1 Doom.
You cannot rest in the Dead or Bleak Forest without setting a guard. Elsewhere, see Setting a Guard. Rest inside an Ordeal’s footprint is never safe, and no Full Rest is possible at the Cause’s site.
  • Repair Items — make a Wits check (+ Repair Focus advantage if held) vs. the item’s Repair BAM! to restore 1 Durability. Requires appropriate tools. Takes 1 Watch per significant repair.
  • Craft (Alchemy) — craft an alchemical item using the Occultism / Alchemy Focus. Requires an Alchemy Kit and appropriate components. 1st Order: 1 Watch. 2nd Order: 2 Watches. 3rd Order: 1 full day. See Alchemy rules.
  • Ritual / Sigil — inscribe a sigil (1 Watch for Glyphs and Wards) or perform an extended magical ritual. See Sigilcraft and Paths Arcane for timing and component requirements.
  • Ordeal Task — once an Ordeal’s Omen has arrived, go to a task’s site and attempt it: play out the task’s encounters, then make the task check (see Ordeals in the Order of Play).

End of Watch Checklist

  • Light. Reduce your Torch die by 1 pip for each Mark (hour) the torch was lit this Watch.
  • Forced March. If you travelled beyond two Watches today, make a Corpus save at the end of each extra Mark. On a failure, add 1 Fatigue and halve your trekking speed from the next Mark until a Full Rest removes that Fatigue. Each Watch of Forced March also costs 1 extra pip of Food and of Water.
  • End of day. If you take a Short or Full Rest this Watch, reduce your Food and Water dice by 1 pip each. A rest taken without both restores nothing (see Food, Water, and Rest).
  • Omen. For each Prepared Challenge or Ordeal, roll 1d6, adding +1 for each Watch it has already waited. On a 6 or higher, its Omen arrives (see Omen Timing). An Ordeal’s Omen roll is made only for Watches spent inside its footprint. When a Major Ordeal’s Omen arrives, every character in the footprint makes a Doom save.
  • Ruin die. At the end of each day of a Minor Ordeal, every 2 days of a Moderate Ordeal, or every 3 days of a Major Ordeal whose Omen has arrived, lower its Ruin die by 1. At 0 the Trouble peaks: roll on the Blight table now.
  • Doom. A Failing character (Doom 1–3) inside an active Ordeal’s footprint makes a Doom save at the end of every Watch.
  • Other triggers. Resolve any other end-of-Watch effect at your location. If an event begins, resolve it before the next Watch. Otherwise, return to step 1 of the Strategic Order of Play.

Ordeals in the Order of Play

A Challenge or Ordeal runs inside the normal loop rather than replacing it. Its full rules are in Challenges and Ordeals and The Fated Road; this is the order in which they come up during play.

  1. Gather Fragments. Explore, Parley with Witnesses, loot, rest inside the footprint (Visions), or spend an Echo to find each Fragment. When all are found, the event is Prepared.
  2. Wait for the Omen. Roll for the Omen at the End of each Watch (see the checklist above). When it arrives, the event begins; for an Ordeal, set its Ruin die to 6.
  3. Work the tasks. Travel to each task’s site, play out its encounters in the Tactical Phase, then make the task check (Ordeal Structure). Every character must be the Lead or a Second in at least one task (Party Size).
  4. Watch for Setbacks. A Setback strikes when a task check fails, any task, Support or decisive check rolls a natural 1, the party loses or flees an encounter inside a task, or the Wyrd die calls for one. Each lowers the Ruin die by 1. A character may spend 1 Boon Point to cancel a Setback as it is rolled; the Ruin die still drops.
  5. Face the Cause. When every task has been attempted, play the Cause encounter (Moderate and Major Ordeals) and make the decisive check. A Major Ordeal’s Cause calls for a Doom save when first faced.
  6. Resolve the outcome. On success, the place changes for good, each character who took a role counts the Ordeal towards their Degrees, and Doom is restored (+1, +2 or +3 by tier; none if the Ruin die reached 0). A Major Ordeal’s Cause Fragment becomes a Thread. On failure, roll a Blight, the Adversary gains a Hold, and every character who took a role loses 1 Doom. On a near miss (failed by 1 or 2, not a natural 1), resolve it as a success, but the Adversary gains a Hold and no Doom is restored.
  7. Advance. A character who gains a Degree restores Doom in full; see Advancement. When a character stands at 9th Degree and the party holds five Threads and the Heart, the Reckoning begins.

Tactical Order of Play

The Tactical Phase begins when an encounter demands immediate interaction. First identify what was encountered and establish approach distance, visibility, and relevant terrain. Determine surprise and disposition before choosing an approach. Roll initiative only when the order of immediate actions matters. Use Encounter scale for approach, long-range exchanges, and pursuit; shift to Combat scale when precise positioning or melee matters (see From Encounter to Combat).

  • determine surprise (opposed Anima check; higher roll acts first and may take a free action),
  • check creature disposition (roll 2d6 if unknown):
    • 2–3 Hostile
    • 4–6 Wary
    • 7–9 Curious
    • 10–11 Friendly
    • 12 Helpful
  • roll initiative (d6 + Anima modifier; if it is a tie, you win)
  • Once the encounter has been initiated, choose one of the following actions:
    • Parley: (not possible if the creature is Hostile or Inimical) You can attempt to communicate, trade, negotiate safe passage, or recruit. Use the Guile skill to shift the creature’s disposition to a more favourable state.
    • Hide: (requires available cover) Make an Anima check opposed by the creature’s Anima (or Average 13) to pass undetected and avoid conflict.
    • Flee: Make an Anima save (Average 13) to break contact. On a failure, each engaged enemy may make one free attack before you move. If an opponent follows, begin a pursuit using the current measured gap, movement mode, terrain, weather, and the Pursuit and Evasion rules in the Travel chapter. Entering a new sub-hex during the pursuit increases its Danger Die by 1. Resolve all flight and pursuit using Pursuit and Evasion. Fleeing an encounter inside an Ordeal task causes a Setback.
    • Ruse: (not available if the creature is Hostile) Attempt to bribe, deceive, or steal before hostilities begin. Opposed Anima check; add Guile rank if held. On a win: the bribe is accepted, the deception succeeds, or the theft goes unnoticed. On a failure: the creature immediately becomes Hostile and initiative is rolled. On a natural 1: the creature gains surprise.
    • Combat: Choose this hostile action when negotiation, concealment, flight, or submission is no longer being attempted. Establish current range and positions, place relevant terrain or obstacles, confirm surprise, and roll initiative. Then use the Combat Round procedure under Tactical Phase. If a character dies, resolve the applicable death or Recursion rules after the encounter, including the final Doom save.
    • Submit: Attempt to appease the creature and avoid hostilities. Make an Anima check (Difficult 16): on a success, forfeit all loot and pass through; on a failure, hostilities continue. Encountered parties may also surrender to avoid conflict, allowing you to move through (though often at the cost of treasure or equipment).
  • Accepting a Temptation encounter calls for a Doom save. Any character may spend 1 Boon Point to reroll one of their d20 rolls, to invoke their Drive for Minor Advantage, or to pass a Critical Damage check automatically.

Encounter Aftermath and Return

When the encounter ends, determine the characters’ final location and the time that passed. Apply damage, Conditions, Fatigue, expended ammunition, Durability loss, consumed items, Mishaps, personal Corruption, Doom, disposition changes, loot, Fragments, and scenario consequences. If the encounter was part of an Ordeal task and the party lost or fled it, a Setback strikes. If movement crossed into a new sub-hex, apply the required Danger or Dread effects. If usable time remains and the interrupted strategic action is still possible, resume it. Otherwise complete the End of Watch procedure and begin the next Watch.

End of Session

  • Doom state. A character who is Tested (Doom 7–9) makes one Doom save at the end of each session.
  • Boon. Unspent Boon Points are lost.
  • Record. Note position, time, resources, Fragments, Prepared events, Omen waits, Ruin dice, the Still Watch count, Doom, Threads and Holds for the next session’s Start of Session.
