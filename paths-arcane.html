Paths Arcane

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

The Arcane Paths form a unified Arcane Framework. Every arcane working uses one casting roll: d20 + Spiritus modifier + the rank of its core skill (Conjuration, Mysticism or Occultism) against the BAM! of the working. Focus ranks never add to the roll; a Focus grants Advantage as its entry states (Competent: Moderate, Accomplished: Major, Adept: Extreme). On a success, pay the cost and apply the effect. On a failure, pay the cost as the table below says and roll on the Magical Misfire table; a failed Healing check rolls only on a natural 1. A natural 1 adds 1 Mishap die to the Misfire roll; a natural 20 follows the Might rules. The three core skills can be loosely defined as the Word (Conjuration: True Names), the Writ (Occultism: Spells, Formulae and Sigils), and the Way (Mysticism: Powers and Mendings).

The Word (Conjuration)

  • True Name working [Conjuration]: compelling, binding, banishing and summoning entities. Cost: Vitality or components. Requires the entity’s True Name (Rebuke excepted).

The Writ (Occultism)

  • Spell [Arcana]: Magic effects that have been written in the language of magic. Cost: Spell Components or Vitality. Requires grimoire.
  • Formula [Alchemy]: crafted consumables. Cost: Components + crafting time. Effect resolves when used, not when crafted.
  • Sigil binding [Sigilcraft]: prepared charged effects. Cost: Components + 1 watch to empower. Limited charges.

The Way (Mysticism)

  • Power [Psionics]: no components, no preparation. Cost: Vitality. Focus Object reduces or eliminates Vitality cost.
  • Mending [Healing]: no Vitality. Cost: Healing Herbs (see Herb Compendium) or a Healing Focus Object. Requires healer’s kit or prepared poultice. Healing Herbs are distinct from Alchemical and Spell Components. All rules specific to each discipline follow in their own sections.

Components vs. Vitality as a Resource

Every arcane discipline has a cost, paid in components, Vitality, or both. Components (such as herbs, powders, and oils) are consumed when an effect is created or used. Alchemy requires specific formulae and ingredients, while Psionics uses no components and draws entirely on the caster’s Vitality. Conjuration and Arcana may use the listed components, a combination of components and Vitality, or Vitality alone. Magic is never risk-free: spending Vitality weakens the caster in combat because Vitality represents health. Each discipline’s cost reflects how readily and reliably its magic can be used.

When costs are paid (whatever the result of the roll):

Arcana spells and Conjuration workings: Vitality and components are paid on a success or a failure.

Psionic powers: Vitality is paid only on a success.

Imbued items (Alchemy and Sigilcraft): components are consumed on a success or a failure; Vitality is paid only on a success.

Healing Arts: the Healing Herb is consumed only on a success.

Exception: a tied Spell Duel refunds both casters’ costs.

Rules for every discipline: Arcana spells and Conjuration workings need speech; a caster who cannot speak cannot cast them (Psionics, Healing and triggering an imbued item need none). If paying a Vitality cost takes you to 0 Vitality, the working still resolves, then make the Critical Damage check at once.

Standard Spells (Arcana)

Casting a spell requires that you have the spell in your grimoire and that it is charged by paying the prescribed cost. Spell costs may be fuelled by one of the following three ways:

  • Using the components as listed,
  • spending Vitality together with fewer components, or
  • spending Vitality alone if no components are available.

Spell costs are paid on a success or a failure. Every spell of an Order costs the same:

  • Cantrips: 1 Vitality and 1 component; or 2 Vitality
  • 1st Order spell: 2 components; or 2 Vitality and 1 component; or 4 Vitality
  • 2nd Order spell: 3 components (minimum 1 Uncommon); or 4 Vitality and 2 components; or 8 Vitality
  • 3rd Order spell: 4 components (minimum 1 Uncommon and 1 Rare); or 6 Vitality and 3 components; or 12 Vitality

Imbued Items (Sigils, Glyphs, Wards, and Alchemical Items)

Sigils, Glyphs, Wards, and Alchemical items are different from spells. They are created once and then work when activated, consumed, or used. The success or failure determination of imbued items occurs at the creation process against the BAM! Rating of imbued item. Imbued items are triggered at will and persisting without further effort, though their creator can only maintain a limited number of imbued items at any given time.

Component costs for imbuing persistent items:

  • 1st Order Glyph, Ward, Sigil, or Alchemical Item: 2 components
  • 2nd Order Glyph, Ward, Sigil, or Alchemical Item: 3 components (minimum 1 Uncommon)
  • 3rd Order Glyph, Ward, Sigil, or Alchemical Item: 4 components (minimum 1 Uncommon and 1 Rare)

The components are consumed as part of the process. Sigils, glyphs and wards take 1 Watch to create at any Order. Alchemical items take 1 Watch at 1st Order, 2 Watches at 2nd Order and 1 day at 3rd Order (as in Playing the Game). On a success, pay the Vitality cost: 2 at 1st Order, 4 at 2nd, 6 at 3rd. On a failure, the components are still consumed, no Vitality is spent, and you roll on the Magical Misfire table.

A practitioner may keep a limited number of active imbued items they created: 3 at Competent, 6 at Accomplished or 9 at Adept, using the higher of their Alchemy and Sigilcraft Focus ranks. This one cap covers every sigil, glyph, ward and alchemical item; individual entries have no limits of their own.

A Note on Crafting Several Items: In one crafting period, a practitioner may create one imbued item per Focus rank (up to three). Each item beyond the first costs +2 Vitality per Order on top of its normal cost (magic is demanding and exacts a price).

■ EXAMPLE: Imbuing a Sigil and Triggering It

The Nameless (Spiritus +1, Occultism Expert +3, Sigilcraft Accomplished, Vitality 16) inscribes a Mark of Warning on their camp threshold. Cost: 2 components (Chalk and Feather), and 2 Vitality on a success. They spend 1 Watch. The Mark is Average (13); Accomplished Sigilcraft gives Major Advantage, two steps easier, so the target is Easy (10). Roll: 9 + 1 + 3 = 13. Success. Vitality 16 → 14. The mark is invisible and active.

Three Watches later, a Ghoul crosses the threshold during the night. The mark rings out: every sleeper within Near range wakes, and the Nameless knows which threshold was crossed. The mark is consumed and must be re-inscribed. Had the crafting check failed, the components would have been spoiled, no Vitality spent, and a Misfire rolled.

■ EXAMPLE: Spending Vitality to Cast

The Nameless (Spiritus +1, Occultism Skilled +2, Vitality 14) casts Frost Bolt (1st Order) with no components, so the cost is 4 Vitality. Roll: d20 + 1 + 2 vs. Average (13). Result: 11 + 3 = 14. Success. Vitality 14 → 10, and the bolt deals 1d6 cold damage. With one Common component (Ice), the cost would have been 2 Vitality and the component. Components matter.

On a failure, the cost is still paid and the Nameless rolls on the Magical Misfire table.

Alchemy

Alchemists use their skills to boost allies, weaken enemies, change bodies, and produce offensive and defensive capabilities to augment the consumer. They make potions, oils, elixirs, powders, and salves that can give benefits, cause harm, or change things in different ways.

To make alchemical items, an Alchemist needs the right ingredients (see Components tables) and must pass an Occultism check against the item’s BAM! (the Alchemy Focus grants Advantage), spend the crafting time to prepare it, and finally imbue the product with their own Vitality to activate it. Most alchemical products are drunk or applied to the skin. Some, like Alchemist’s Fire or Acid, can be thrown up to Near range (25 m). Healing Herbs (see Herb Compendium) are not alchemical ingredients and can only be used in recipes if specifically listed as both a herb and a component.

Stacking brews: drinking a second alchemical item before the first has expired risks a Magical Misfire (see The Alchemist in Playing the Game).

■ EXAMPLE: Crafting an Alchemical Item

The Nameless (Spiritus +0, Occultism Skilled +2, Alchemy Competent) brews a Quickening Salve (2nd Order, Difficult 16). They have Claw (Common), Mercury (Uncommon) and a Magic Feather (Uncommon), and spend 2 Watches in camp. Competent Alchemy gives Moderate Advantage, so the target drops one step to Average (13).

Roll: d20 + 0 (Spiritus) + 2 (Occultism) vs. 13. Result: 9 + 2 = 11. Failure. The components are spoiled, no Vitality is spent, and they roll on the Magical Misfire table. They must find more before trying again.

Next attempt, with fresh components: 15 + 2 = 17. Success. They pay 4 Vitality and the salve is ready. Used in combat, it grants one extra action per round and half again the movement rate for 1d6 rounds, then a Corpus save (Average 13) or 1 Fatigue marker.

Alchemists’ products are one of the following categories:

  • Elixir: a thick, tarry liquid (requires a sap or tar base). Can be applied over an area. Can be thrown if in a vial up to Near range (25 m)
  • Oil: a viscous fluid suspension (requires an oil base). Can be applied topically, ingested, spread over an area, or thrown if in a vial
  • Potion: a liquid suspension (requires a liquid base such as water, wine, or blood). Usually ingested but can be applied over an object or area. Can be sprayed from a container up to Near range (25 m) or thrown in a vial up to Near Range.
  • Powder: a fine dusty substance (requires a dry solid base ground into a powder). Can be used to coat an object or area. Can be thrown at Close range (3 m) or further if blown by wind (up to Near range until dispersed)
  • Salt: a crystalline substance coarser than powder (requires a salt base). Can be spread over an object, creature or area. Can be thrown at Close range (3 m) or further if blown by wind (up to Near range until dispersed)
  • Salve: a fatty spreadable compound (requires a lipid base such as tallow or wax). Can be applied topically to a creature or object. Cannot be thrown.

Arcana

The Occultism skill grants the rudiment abilities in the arcane arts and unlocks the ability to understand and use spell magic. Spellcasters cast advanced spells via the Arcana focus. The casting of spells requires the expenditure of energy, be it personal energy through the expenditure of Vitality or using spell components (or both). While practitioners begin with a grimoire that contains some basic spells, more advanced spells are closely guarded and highly sought out by practitioners – often the items of quests and the drive for adventure.

All practitioners of the arcane arts have the following cantrips available as soon as they gain the Occultism skill:

Detect Magic

enables the caster to detect if an item or area is under a magic affect. It does not grant the ability to determine what the effect is.

  • BAM!: Easy (10)
  • Duration: immediate
  • Range: Far (100 m)
  • Cost: 1 Vitality and 1 component; or 2 Vitality
  • Spell Component: Bone chips (consumed on casting)
  • Damage/Effect: You sense whether any magic is active within range. You learn if an item, creature, or area is under a magical effect, but not what the effect is or its source. Multiple active effects are detected as a single presence.

Read Magic

enables the caster to read the basic magic texts that have not been warded (protected)

  • BAM!: Easy (10)
  • Duration: 1 Tick
  • Range: Near (25 m)
  • Cost: 1 Vitality and 1 component; or 2 Vitality
  • Spell Component: Glass shard (consumed on casting)
  • Damage/Effect: You can read warded, magical, or arcane script that would otherwise be incomprehensible. For the duration, runes, sigils, spell scrolls, and protected magical texts are legible to you. This does not reveal the meaning of unknown languages, only magical encoding.

Mage Candle

creates a dim light, like a candle, just bright enough to read by. It hovers above the caster and lights a 3-metre radius.

  • BAM!: Easy (10)
  • Duration: 6 Ticks
  • Range: Near (25 m)
  • Cost: 1 Vitality and 1 component; or 2 Vitality
  • Spell Component: Sulphur (consumed on casting)
  • Damage/Effect: A small flame springs into being and hovers just above your head, shedding dim light in a 3-metre radius — enough to read by. The candle moves with you and cannot be blown out by natural wind, but it does not shed enough light to eliminate penalties for darkness in combat. It emits no heat and cannot ignite flammable materials.

Spell Duels

A Spell Duel occurs when two practitioners bring conflicting magical workings to bear on each other directly — one caster countering another's spell, both targeting each other simultaneously, or one practitioner attempting to seize control of another's active magical effect. A Spell Duel is not a separate minigame: it is resolved in the same round as normal combat, using the same d20 roll as casting, extended by a single opposed check.

When a Spell Duel Occurs

A Spell Duel is triggered when:

  • Counterspell: A practitioner declares they are countering a spell they can see being cast within Near range (25 m). This declaration must be made after the casting is announced but before the original caster rolls. Counterspelling uses the responding caster’s Reaction for the round.
  • Mutual Attack: Both practitioners cast spells targeting each other in the same round. Both declare their spells, then both resolve simultaneously as a Spell Duel rather than as separate rolls.
  • Contested Effect: One caster attempts to seize control of another's summoned entity, dispel an active ward or binding, or override an ongoing effect that was placed by another practitioner. The original caster may oppose the attempt even if not present — the working itself resists disruption.

Resolving a Spell Duel

Both sides make an Arcane Contest roll: d20 + Spiritus modifier + the rank of the core skill used for the working (Conjuration, Mysticism or Occultism). A relevant Focus grants Advantage as usual. Cantrips used to counter add only the Spiritus modifier with no skill rank bonus.

Compare the results:

  • Higher roll wins outright. The winner's spell resolves normally against its target (which may be the loser). The loser's spell is cancelled and their resource cost (Vitality or components) is consumed regardless.
  • Equal roll: both effects cancel and both resource costs are refunded (the one exception to the usual cost rules). Neither spell took effect. Describe the magical energies meeting and dissipating — a visible flash, a crack of displaced air, the smell of burnt ozone.
  • Lower roll loses. The winner's spell resolves. The loser's spell fails and their cost is consumed. Additionally, the losing practitioner must make a Spiritus save (BAM!: Average 13) or take 1d3 Vitality damage from magical backlash as the thwarted working rebounds.

Counterspell vs. Mutual Attack

These two modes produce different outcomes on a win:

  • Counterspell (you used your Reaction): If you win, the opponent's spell is cancelled. Your Reaction is spent, but you keep your own action. If you lose, the opponent’s spell resolves and your Reaction is wasted.
  • Mutual Attack (both casting): If you win, your spell resolves against the opponent and their spell is cancelled. If you lose, their spell resolves against you and yours is cancelled. On a tie, both cancel. The risk is higher but so is the potential — a winning Mutual Attack deals your spell's full effect on the opponent without giving them a save based on spell targeting (though saves against specific effects still apply).

Modifiers to the Arcane Contest Roll

  • +3: Caster is inside a prepared Summoning Circle.
  • +3: Countering with a spell of equal or higher Order than the spell being opposed (e.g. countering a 2nd Order spell with a 2nd or 3rd Order spell).
  • +3: Caster has an active Psionic Ward or equivalent protective effect.
  • -3: Countering with a cantrip against an ordered spell (1st, 2nd, or 3rd Order).
  • -3: Caster is under any Condition that would affect concentration (Dizzied, Confused, Frightened).
  • -3: Caster is attempting to oppose a Glyph, Ward, or Sigil without physically touching the inscription surface. These items resist disruption — they were designed for it.

Natural 1s and Natural 20s

A natural 1 on the Arcane Contest roll is an automatic loss regardless of modifiers and triggers an immediate Magical Misfire (roll on the Magical Misfire table) in addition to the normal Spell Duel outcome. The backlash save is still required and made at Moderate Disadvantage.

A natural 20 is an automatic win regardless of the opponent's total. On a natural 20, the losing caster must additionally make a Spiritus save vs. Difficult (16) or suffer the Locked condition (see Special Conditions in Character Rules) from the force of the magical disruption.

Duelling Over Multiple Rounds

A Spell Duel resolves in a single round. If both practitioners are still standing afterward, they may continue casting and opposing each other in subsequent rounds — each round is its own Spell Duel if both choose to engage that way. There is no automatic chaining mechanic: each round, each caster decides independently whether to attack, counter, move, or do something else entirely. The Bleak Forest does not reward standing in place trading blows with a Lych.

Example: The Nameless (Occultism Master +4, Spiritus +1) spots a Hag beginning to cast a hex and counterspells with their Reaction. Both roll: the Nameless 14 + 1 + 4 = 19; the Hag 11 + 1 + 2 = 14. The Nameless wins and the hex is cancelled. The Hag’s 4 Vitality is still spent. The Nameless still has their own action this round.

Conjuration

Conjuration is the practice of compelling, binding, banishing, and summoning entities of supernatural origin (undead, fae, demonic, etc.). It is the most dangerous branch of the arcane arts for the practitioner, because unlike Arcana or Sigilcraft; Conjuration works directly upon beings that have their own will and every working risks retaliation, contamination, or betrayal.

True Names

A True Name uniquely identifies a supernatural being, representing the core resonant syllables tied to its existence. This is not just any name it uses or is given; True Names are bound up in the very fabric of the being’s essence. When you speak an entity’s True Name in its presence, it must respond; it cannot ignore you, feign ignorance, or easily mislead you about what it truly is. There are two types of True Name: Nominal and Resonant. Nominal True Names (as the moniker would imply) define some aspects of the being in question but are less specific in nature and do not have as strong effect as a Resonant True Name.

If you possess no True Name for a being, you cannot compel, bind, banish or summon it; you can only Rebuke it. Depending on its demeanour, it either ignores you or becomes Hostile.

A Nominal True Name allows you to force an interaction as though you possessed the being’s Resonant True Name. However, every Action made to bind, exorcise, summon, command, or otherwise deal with it is made at a Major Disadvantage against the creature’s BAM!. Ranks in the relevant Binding, Exorcism, or Summoning Focus reduce this Disadvantage as specified by that Focus.

True Name required, by Entity Tier: Tier 0 and Tier I (Might 0–4): a Nominal or Resonant True Name. Tier II (Might 5–7): the Resonant True Name. Tier III (Might 8+): the Resonant True Name and a prepared summoning circle. With no True Name: Rebuke Entity only.

How to Discover a True Name: You can't create or guess a True Name; you must learn it by:

  • Research: Scouring codices, inscriptions, or ancient texts (Wits check, BAM! based on entity's Might: Easy for Might 0–4, Average for Might 5–7, Difficult for Might 8+; requires at least one watch of study).

  • Coercion: Forcing a bound lesser entity of the same type to reveal it (Conjuration check against that entity’s BAM!).

  • Revelation: The entity or another knowledgeable creature freely gives the name, often at great cost.

  • Encounter: Some entities blurt out their names in anger or pride. Hearing it spoken means you know it.

How to Use a True Name: To Banish, Bind or Summon an entity, you must clearly say its Nominal or Resonant True Name aloud (it cannot be written or mouthed). When a True Name is spoken while the entity is in the same hex, it makes a Spiritus save against the speaker’s Conjuration check result (minimum Average 13) or is compelled to approach the speaker within one round. This does not necessarily make it hostile, but it will know who called its name.

Summoning Circles

A summoning circle is a protective ward made with ash, chalk, coarse salt, ash, or carved into stone. Preparing one correctly takes 1 watch. When you perform Conjuration inside a properly prepared circle:

  • You gain Moderate Advantage on all Conjuration checks.
  • A Summoned creature appears outside the circle and cannot cross its boundary unless the caster allows it.
  • If a Binding is broken, the circle keeps the creature contained for 1 more round before it fails.

If someone breaks the circle while a creature is bound, make an immediate Spiritus save against Difficult (16). On a failure, the creature is freed and becomes Hostile.

A normal circle stops working if its markings are disturbed, such as when someone walks through it or water washes it away.

You can carve a permanent circle into stone with a Challenging (19) Wits check and 1 full day of work. A permanent circle is not damaged by weather or normal contact.

■ EXAMPLE: Summoning with and without a True Name

Without a True Name: The Nameless wants to summon a Draugr (Might 0, Tier 0, BAM! Average 13) but has no True Name for it. They cannot summon it at all; if one appears, they can only Rebuke it.

With a True Name and a circle: The Nameless researches the Draugr’s True Name (1 Watch, Wits check Easy 10) and prepares a summoning circle (1 Watch). The circle grants Moderate Advantage, so Average (13) becomes Easy (10). Cost: 4 Vitality. Roll: 8 + 0 (Spiritus) + 3 (Conjuration Expert) = 11. Success. The Draugr appears at the circle’s edge, constrained, and its disposition is checked. The Nameless immediately casts Bind Entity, still inside the circle, to hold it before negotiating.

Preparation is not caution. It is survival.

Entity Tiers and Conjuration

Entities are categorised by Might into four tiers for Conjuration purposes (the Might tiers in Character Rules):

  • Tier 0 (Might 0): human-scale entities such as draugr, imps, lesser undead and minor fae. A Nominal True Name is enough.
  • Tier I (Might 1–4): imposing entities such as a death knight or a revenant. A Nominal True Name is enough, at Major Disadvantage (see above).
  • Tier II (Might 5–7): significant entities such as greater demons, lyches, vampires and Sidhe nobles. Their Resonant True Name is required for binding or summoning.
  • Tier III (Might 8+) covers beings that defy mortal limits: demon lords, ancient undead, true elementals, and entities whose natures are not fully understood. Binding a Tier III entity requires a prepared summoning circle AND its Resonant True Name; even then, the binding lasts only 1 watch regardless of Vitality spent. Summoning a Tier III entity without both is not possible (the ritual simply fails).

Consequences of Failed Conjuration

Failed Conjuration workings pose a real danger to the caster. On a failed check, roll on the Magical Misfire table as normal. Additionally: if the entity was present or summoned and the check fails, the entity is immediately free of any compulsion and knows who attempted to bind it. Entities of Might 5+ that were subject to a failed binding will not simply leave, they will act against the practitioner on their next turn. A natural 1 on any Conjuration working in the presence of a summoned entity causes the entity to make a free attack before the Mishap roll is made. The Magical Misfire table result is applied in addition, not instead. There is no safe practice of Conjuration. There is only careful practice.

  • BAM!: Based on the BAM! of the entity

A Bind Entity cast immediately after a won Spiritus contest is made at Major Advantage (not automatically successful). Summoning needs the True Name its tier requires; without it, the working fails.

  • BAM!: Based on the BAM! of the entity. True Name not required.

  • Cost: 2 Vitality
  • Spell Component: Handful of coarse salt (consumed on casting); True Name NOT required.

The entity is not destroyed and is not prevented from returning after the duration. Entities of Might 5+ make a Spiritus save at casting BAM! to resist. On a natural 20, the entity is driven back for 1 watch instead. Rebuke does not require the True Name and can be used in desperation — but unlike Banish, it is temporary and leaves the entity present in the hex, merely distant.

  • BAM!: Might 0–4: Average (13); Might 5–7: Difficult (16); Might 8+: Challenging (19). True Name required.

It may answer obliquely, speak in riddles, or provide technically true but misleading answers — the compulsion is to truth, not clarity. The entity knows it was compelled and remembers. Focus Object Creation (Mysticism)

A Focus Object (Healing or Psionic) is more than a component: it is a vessel holding part of the caster's life. Unlike a sigil or potion, the Vitality spent to create one is a permanent sacrifice that reduces the caster's maximum Vitality until the Focus Object is destroyed or relinquished.

To create a Focus Object, the caster must complete 1 Watch of uninterrupted ritual work, provide the appropriate materials (1 Uncommon component), succeed on a check with the discipline’s core skill vs. Difficult (16), and permanently sacrifice Vitality to attune it. On failure, the materials are consumed and the Vitality is spent, but that Vitality returns as normal instead of becoming permanent.

A Focus Object can substitute for the Vitality cost of one use per Order level per watch: once for a 1st Order power, once for a 2nd Order power, and once for a 3rd Order power. After substitution, that Order's slot is spent until the next watch period. If the owner takes Critical Damage while channelling through the Focus Object, it is destroyed.

Vitality cost for Focus Object creation

Base cost: two-thirds of the caster's current maximum Vitality, rounded up. Example: maximum Vitality 15 gives a base cost of 10.

Mysticism skill rank cost reduction: subtract (skill rank × 2) from the base Vitality cost. Minimum Vitality cost is always 4 Vitality, regardless of rank.

Example: costs by rank (caster with maximum Vitality 18, base cost 12):

  • Apprentice (rank 1): 12 − 2 = 10 Vitality
  • Skilled (rank 2): 12 − 4 = 8 Vitality
  • Expert (rank 3): 12 − 6 = 6 Vitality
  • Master (rank 4): 12 − 8 = 4 Vitality (minimum)
  • Grand Master (rank 5): 12 − 10 = 4 Vitality (minimum)

The sacrificed Vitality reduces the caster's maximum Vitality until the Focus Object is relinquished (given to another) or destroyed. The caster then restores maximum Vitality at 1 point per full day of rest. A caster may hold only one Focus Object per arcane discipline at a time. Creating a second Focus Object in the same discipline destroys the first, releasing the bound Vitality immediately.

This high cost is intentional: veteran practitioners, whose skill rank can minimise it, are the ones most likely to maintain Focus Objects. A Focus Object found on a dead caster is a valuable prize because its power is real and was paid for in life.

A Note on Time Spent Crafting and Resting:

Time spent crafting imbued items or researching does not count as resting or as any other activity, such as foraging. While imbuing items, you gain no rest benefits for that watch; treat it as a watch spent travelling, because arcane work is physically and mentally taxing.

Healing Arts

The Healing Arts use focused intention, prepared Healing Herbs, and direct touch to repair the body, mind, and spirit. Unlike Alchemy, Healing Arts restore the body to its natural state; they do not buff, augment, or transform. Instead, each act of healing consumes a Healing Herb (see Herb Compendium); the Healing Arts never cost Vitality. Healing Herbs are distinct from Alchemical Components: they are gathered by foraging with Herb Search intent, purchased from Herbalists, or harvested from specific creatures. They cannot substitute for Alchemical Components in formulae, and Alchemical Components cannot substitute for Healing Herbs in the Healing Arts.

Resolution: roll d20 + Spiritus modifier + Mysticism skill rank vs. the BAM! listed for each power (the Healing Focus grants Advantage). On a success, consume the herb and apply the effect. On a failure, no herb is consumed and the attempt simply fails; on a natural 1, roll on the Magical Misfire table.

A healer’s kit (1 inventory slot, 2sp) is required for 2nd Order and 3rd Order work; 1st Order healing can be performed with a Healing Herb alone.

At Adept rank, the Focus Object replaces all Healing Herb costs — no herb is consumed. Below Adept, the Focus Object grants Moderate Advantage on all healing checks.

Psionics

Psionics is the art of channelling raw mental and spiritual force without external components. Unlike Arcana, psionic powers require no grimoire, no spell components, and no preparation. Psionics are the pure expressions of inner will acting directly upon the world. This freedom comes at a significant cost: psionic powers are far more draining than equivalent spells.

Casting a psionic power works like casting a spell: roll d20 + Spiritus modifier + Mysticism skill rank vs. the power’s BAM!. On a failure, no Vitality is spent, but roll on the Magical Misfire table. On a success, the power takes effect and you pay the Vitality cost. Powers that allow a target to resist use an Anima save (for mental effects) or a Corpus save (for physical effects) at the BAM! listed in the power’s entry.

Psionic powers are divided into three Orders matching the structure of Arcana:

  • 1st Order — Average (13): Minor sensory and kinetic effects. Range: Near to Far.
  • 2nd Order — Difficult (16): Significant mental, physical, and pyrokinetic effects. Range: Near to Far.
  • 3rd Order — Challenging (19): Transformative or devastating effects. Range: Near to Span.

Vitality Cost and the Psionic Toll

Because psionic powers require no components and no preparation, they are available at any moment — but the body pays directly. Base Vitality costs are higher than equivalent Arcana spells:

  • 1st Order: 4 Vitality
  • 2nd Order: 8 Vitality
  • 3rd Order: 12 Vitality

On a failed casting check, no Vitality is paid but the Magical Misfire table is consulted as normal. If the caster is reduced to 0 Vitality mid-casting (from paying the cost), the power still resolves but a Critical Damage Check is required immediately after.

Psionics Focus

The Psionics Focus (available through the Mysticism skill) represents disciplined mental training and focused will. Each focus rank reduces the Vitality cost of psionic powers. Additionally, Adept practitioners may use a Psionic Focus Object instead of paying Vitality.

  • Competent: 1st Order 4 Vitality, 2nd Order 7 Vitality, 3rd Order 10 Vitality
  • Accomplished: 1st Order 3 Vitality, 2nd Order 6 Vitality, 3rd Order 9 Vitality
  • Adept: costs as Accomplished, or substitute a Psionic Focus Object for the Vitality cost (see below)

Even at Accomplished, a psionic power costs more Vitality than the same Order of spell paid with components. The trade-off is freedom: psionics needs no components, grimoire or preparation.
