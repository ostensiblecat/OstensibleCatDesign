Oracles

An oracle answers a question that no other rule answers. Solo and Co-Op players use them in place of a Narrator; a Narrator can use them for a surprise. Ask the most specific rule first: Omens, weather, reactions, Setbacks and the encounter tables all come before these. Use these tables when nothing else fits, such as “Is the ferryman lying?” or “Is the door barred?”.

The Yes or No Oracle

  1. Ask a question that can be answered yes or no.
  2. Choose how likely a yes is, from what the characters know and what the story so far suggests.
  3. Roll 1d20. Meet or beat the target and the answer is yes.
How likely Yes on “Yes, but” “No, but”
Near certain 4 4–5 2–3
Likely 7 7–8 5–6
Even 11 11–12 9–10
Unlikely 14 14–15 12–13
Very unlikely 17 17–18 15–16
  • Yes, but and No, but are results just either side of the target: the answer holds, with a twist or a cost.
  • A natural 20 is “Yes, and”: more than you asked for. A natural 1 is “No, and”: worse than a plain no.
  • Doom leans on the oracle. When a yes would help a character who is Failing (Doom 1–3), treat the question as one step less likely (see Doom and Boon).
  • Ask once. Do not ask the same question again until something in the fiction has changed.
Example: Wren asks: “Is the ferryman lying about the toll?” He has been helpful, so she calls it Unlikely. The d20 shows 14: “Yes, but”. He is lying, but only because the Dykemasters set the toll, and he is afraid of them.

Meaning Tables

When an answer needs more than yes or no, such as what a stranger wants or what the Trouble in a village looks like, roll on both tables (d66: roll two d6, the first for the row, the second for the column) and read the pair as a prompt. The first idea that fits is usually the right one.

People on the Fly

For a person the characters meet who has no stat block, roll 1d6 on each column, then roll their disposition as usual. Use Fooled Folk or a Retainer if you need numbers.

1d6 Who Wants Fears or hides Manner
1 A farmer or herder To be left alone The Forest and what walks in it Curt and suspicious
2 A pedlar or carter Coin, food or safety for their own Their master, lord or guild Warm, too warm
3 A priest, monk or hermit Revenge on someone nearby Being found out: they hide a small sin Frightened and talkative
4 A guard, reeve or bailiff To get out: of a debt, a place or a bargain Being found out: they serve the Adversary Proud and slow to trust
5 A crafter: smith, miller, weaver To be believed Losing someone who is already ill or missing Tired beyond caring
6 A noble, steward or guild officer Something the characters have Nothing yet, and that is about to change Pious and full of omens

A name: roll 1d6 twice and join the parts: 1 Al, 2 Bran, 3 Cor, 4 Ed, 5 Gar, 6 Hal; then 1 a, 2 ric, 3 wen, 4 mund, 5 is, 6 eth. A first roll of 2 and a second of 3 give Branwen.

Rumours

Roll d66 when the characters gather rumours, share a fire with travellers, or need a hook. Then roll 1d6 to see what the Rumour is worth:

  • 1–2: false, or so twisted it misleads.
  • 3–5: true, but it is local colour, or the start of a Challenge.
  • 6: true, and it points at the Fated Road: if the nearest Ordeal is not yet Prepared, the Rumour reveals the form of its next missing Fragment, as an Omen would (see Fragment forms).
1st d6 2nd: 1 2 3 4 5 6
1 Someone in the next village has stopped casting a shadow. The ferryman will not cross after dark any more, and will not say why. A trader paid in coin that turned to leaves by morning. The old road north is safer than the new one. Or the reverse. Wolves were seen walking upright at the edge of the fields. A healer in town cures anything, for a price no one will name.
2 Bells were heard under the lake. Three children came home from the woods, and one of them is not the same. The lord’s men are taking a tithe that was never owed. There is treasure in the barrow on the hill, and a curse with it. A pilgrim saw the same stranger in four towns in four days. The well water tastes of iron since the storm.
3 A knight in strange colours is asking after the characters by name. The mill has ground nothing but bone meal for a week. An abbey on the moor has shut its gates to everyone. The crows have left the valley. A merchant is selling maps of the Forest that show a path no one has walked. The priest preached a sermon no one remembers, and everyone obeys it.
4 Graves in the churchyard have been opened from the inside. A fire burns on the ridge each night, and no one tends it. The bridge toll has doubled, and the toll-keeper is new. Something has been taking sheep, but leaving the bones neatly stacked. A woman claims she has lived this day before. Hunters have stopped going past the standing stone.
5 The town’s guild has struck a bargain with someone outside the walls. A girl hums a song that makes listeners weep and sleep. Salt has gone up tenfold, and the salters are afraid. The dead are walking the Corpse Road again. A ruined tower has a light in its window. Travellers say the river ran backwards for an hour.
6 The reeve has hanged a man who is still seen at the tavern. An old map shows a village here that no one remembers. Fae have been seen dancing at the crossroads. A smith forged a blade that will not stop bleeding. A beggar knows things no beggar should. Someone is paying good silver for news of the characters.

Keeping the Road Secret

Each Goal’s tables give away its Causes, its False Fragments and its Adversary to whoever reads them. In Solo and Co-Op play the players hold those tables, so read them the way a Narrator would reveal them: one piece at a time.

  1. Roll first, read second. Roll the Ordeal, then read only the row you rolled.
  2. Read only what is revealed. Read the place and the Omen when the Ordeal is seeded. Read a task or the Cause only when its Fragment is found. Leave the rest covered, with a card or a sheet of paper.
  3. Hide which Fragments are false. Do not read whether a Fragment is false when you find it. Record it with a question mark, and learn the truth only by testing it or at the decisive check.
  4. Let the generator keep secrets. The Ordeal Generator can hide what the Fragments have not yet revealed; use it wherever you can.
  5. Keep the Adversary hidden until the first Thread names it.

In Co-Op play, the Keeper of the Road does the reading.

A Solo Session

  1. Pick up the thread. Read your log from last time: where you are, what you hold, which Ordeal is Prepared.
  2. Play the Watches. Follow the Order of Play: the rules and tables run the world.
  3. Ask when you need to. When a question has no rule, ask the oracle. When the answer needs colour, roll on the meaning tables.
  4. Decide the small things. If an answer would not change anything that matters, just choose it. Save the dice for questions with stakes.
  5. Keep a log. A line for each Watch is enough: what you did, what you found, what it cost. Note every Fragment, Rumour and name.
  6. Stop at a Watch’s end, and write one line on what you mean to do next.
