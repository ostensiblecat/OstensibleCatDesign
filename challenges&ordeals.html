Bleak Forest

Tabletop RPG System

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

Challenges and Ordeals drive the game towards fulfilment of the cycle. Fragments make an event ready; an Omen says when it starts. This replaces the Challenge Track. Sisters’ Omens are simply Omens and follow the same rules as any other Omen.

Challenges are the personal trials at the heart of Escape the Bleak Forest. Presented by the Three Wyrd Sisters, they test Corpus, Anima, or Spiritus and determine who the Nameless becomes through Recursion. A Challenge is tied to an attribute because its reward Locks that attribute. It tests who the character is and what they will become.

A successful Challenge Locks the tested attribute and grants an Identity Mark. The Maiden offers a Vow or Knot after a Minor Challenge, the Matron offers a Vow or Scar after a Moderate Challenge, and the Crone offers a Scar or Knot after a Major Challenge. A Challenge therefore changes more than an attribute; it establishes a promise the character must uphold, a lasting wound they must carry, or a survival lesson they can call upon.

Ordeals carry that completed identity into Bleak Forest: Hail Three Wyrd Sisters. They are wider scenarios in which the character’s Vows, Scars, and Knots are tested against problems affecting a place, community, or region. Ordeals do not replace the character’s Identity Marks. They create situations in which a Vow’s constraint matters, a Scar complicates or illuminates action, and a Knot provides a learned response.

An Ordeal has an overall BAM! for its decisive resolution and is built from 2–4 tasks rolled against attributes, covering at least two different attributes. This ensures that every character has a way into the Ordeal and allows Identity Marks to matter at the task level. Completing an Ordeal changes the world, advances Degrees of the Road, and may also deepen, reinterpret, or add to the character’s Identity Marks when its outcome calls for it.

Both Challenges and Ordeals have Minor, Moderate, and Major tiers, but they operate at different scales. A Challenge is a test of becoming: Minor is Average (13), Moderate is Difficult (16), and Major is Challenging (19). An Ordeal is a problem in the world whose decisive resolution ranges from Difficult (16) to Formidable (22). Its encounters, discoveries, negotiations, and tasks build towards that resolution.

Throughout either event, apply the character’s Identity Marks whenever their triggers, benefits, or constraints are relevant.

  • Vows: A Vow is a personal law with a benefit and a constraint. Its benefit applies when relevant, but the character must honour its constraint during a Challenge or Ordeal. If an event makes two Vows incompatible, choose which to honour and resolve the consequences of breaking the other under the rules of the game being played.
  • Scars: A Scar is a permanent wound, burden, or hard-won insight. Apply its complication whenever the fiction invokes it. When a Scar gives the character special perspective, permission, or an applicable benefit, it may also open an approach that would otherwise be unavailable. A Scar is not removed by success, though its meaning may change.
  • Knots: A Knot is a learned survival response with a specific trigger and limit. It may be used during a Challenge or during an appropriate task within an Ordeal. Using a Knot can change how a task is attempted, but it does not by itself complete the entire event unless that event explicitly says so.
This page holds the full rules for Challenges and Ordeals. The Fated Road uses them as written and adds the campaign around them: the Goal, its Adversary, Holds, Threads and the Reckoning. Where these rules mention the Goal tables, they mean the Ordeal entries of a Fated Road Goal; an Ordeal off the road is rolled on the Ordeal Generator instead (see Building Challenges and Ordeals, which also holds worked examples), and wherever the rules name the Adversary, it means whoever stands behind that Ordeal’s Cause.

Fragments, Preparation, and Omens

The cycle of a Challenge or Ordeal follows four steps:

  1. Find Fragments. Each Fragment is a clue tied to one named Challenge or Ordeal.
  2. Become Prepared. When the event holds the required number of Fragments, it becomes Prepared.
  3. Receive an Omen. The world signals that the Prepared event begins, usually establishing when or where it starts.
  4. Resolve the Event. Face the Challenge or Ordeal. Apply its rewards on success, or its Blight on refusal or failure.

Fragments

A Fragment is a piece of evidence tied to one named Challenge or Ordeal. Record each Fragment against its event. Fragments never transfer between events.

  • Challenge Fragments reveal, in order, the trial, the site, the stakes and the past (see the Challenge Generator’s Fragments step).
  • Task Fragment: reveals one task of an Ordeal: what must be done, and at which site.
  • Cause Fragment: found last; reveals the Ordeal’s hidden Cause. On the Fated Road, the Cause Fragment of a Major Ordeal is also a Thread.

Fragment forms

Every Fragment has a form, and the form says how it is gained. An Ordeal from the Goal tables lists each Fragment’s form; otherwise roll 1d6 for each one.

1d6 Form Gained by
1 Relic: an object from the site Explore or loot
2 Witness: a creature that saw something An encounter or Parley
3 Mark: a sign carved, burned or left behind Explore (Wits, Difficult 16)
4 Rumour: a story from a traveller or wanderer A Special Event or Parley
5 Vision: a dream during rest A Short or Full Rest (for an Ordeal, inside its footprint)
6 Echo: a memory from a past Recursion An Echo spend, or recalling a death

Fragments can also turn up through Rare Encounters, loot and Lore. Exploring for a Fragment is a Wits check (Difficult 16) unless another rule says otherwise. A Fragment found before its event is known can be named with a Lore check (Difficult 16). A Fragment for an Ordeal one tier above the characters’ band can be found and recorded, but that Ordeal cannot begin until it is Prepared.

Order of discovery. The Goal tables and the generators list Fragments in the order that makes most sense, but the players can find them in any order, except that an Ordeal’s Cause Fragment is always last. If the players reach the Cause Fragment’s place early, they find signs of it but not the Fragment itself. Where an Ordeal’s Fragments lie is set out under Ordeal Scale, and some of them are false: see Red Herrings.

Prepared

An event becomes Prepared when it holds its required number of true Fragments.

Tier Challenge Fragments Ordeal Tasks Ordeal Fragments
Minor 2 2 3
Moderate 3 3 4
Major 4 4 5

An Ordeal therefore always requires a number of Fragments equal to its number of tasks +1. One Fragment reveals each task, while the final Fragment reveals the underlying Cause. A Prepared event does not begin immediately. It waits for an Omen.

Omens

An Omen is a sign that a Prepared Challenge or Ordeal begins: for an Ordeal, the moment the Trouble breaks into the open. An Ordeal from the Goal tables has its own Omen; the generators roll one. Omens may come from:

  • Danger Die Special results
  • Sisters’ Omens
  • scenario triggers
  • an event’s own Omen
  • the Omen timing roll

When an Omen occurs for an event that is Prepared, begin the event at the time and place indicated by the Omen. Where an Ordeal’s Omen falls, and what happens if the characters are elsewhere, is set out under Ordeal Scale.

If the event is not yet Prepared, the Omen does not begin it. Instead, it reveals the form of the event’s next missing Fragment.

Omen Timing

Once an event is Prepared, roll 1d6 at the end of each Watch, adding +1 for each such Watch already waited since it became Prepared. On 6 or more, its Omen arrives. For an Ordeal, count only the Watches spent inside its footprint.

Danger Die Special results, Sisters’ Omens and scenario triggers can bring an Omen sooner, without waiting for this roll.

Taint

Every Ordeal’s Cause leaves a Taint: a smell, a sign or a wrongness that marks everything it touches. The Taint is there from the first scene. It shows in the signs of the Trouble, on every Fragment, in the Omen and at the Cause encounter, so the players always know when they are on this Ordeal’s trail.

A Taint never names the Cause. Each one fits three Causes, so it narrows the hunt without ending it, and only the Cause Fragment reveals the Cause. The characters know the old signs as well as anyone, so share the “Could mean” column with the players freely.

  • Finding the Taint. The Ordeal Generator rolls it from the Cause (see step 4b). When you write an Ordeal yourself, choose a Taint whose “Could mean” includes its Cause.
  • False Fragments carry it too. A Taint proves a Fragment belongs to this Ordeal, not that it is true (see Red Herrings).
  • A spreading Cause keeps its Taint. When a Blight spreads the Cause to a new place, the new Ordeal shows the same Taint.
  • On the Fated Road, a Goal’s own Ordeals also carry the Goal’s signature Taint, the mark of its Adversary.
1d12 Taint Could mean
1 Barking dogs: dogs that will not stop barking after dark Greed or fear, Restless dead, Demonic
2 Turned earth: fresh-dug earth where no one admits to digging Greed or fear, Restless dead, Elemental
3 Unclaimed smoke: smoke from a fire no one admits to Greed or fear, Demonic, Elemental
4 Nailed charms: charms nailed over doors, and prayers whispered Greed or fear, Fae bargain, Demonic
5 Soured milk: milk soured and bread mouldered overnight Fae bargain, Demonic, Abrikoth
6 Rings: rings of mushrooms or stones Fae bargain, Restless dead, Elemental
7 Rust: iron rusting overnight Fae bargain, Elemental, Abrikoth
8 Lost time: candles burnt too far, meals gone cold, hours no one remembers Fae bargain, Restless dead, Abrikoth
9 Cold spots: cold spots, and breath fogging indoors Restless dead, Elemental, Abrikoth
10 Crows: crows gathering while the songbirds fall silent Restless dead, Demonic, Abrikoth
11 Misplaced coin: coin where it should not be: in wells, on graves, in cradles Greed or fear, Fae bargain, Abrikoth
12 Water gone wrong: brackish wells and streams running red Greed or fear, Demonic, Elemental

Outside Hail Three Wyrd Sisters there is no Abrikoth’s hand, so some Taints point to only two Causes.

Deepening Taint

For a Major Ordeal, its Omen brings a second Taint, found the same way (reroll a repeat). From then on the Ordeal shows both. Two Taints together usually leave one or two Causes, so the hunt sharpens just as the Trouble breaks into the open.

Red Herrings

The Adversary does not want to be found, so every Ordeal hides false clues among the true ones. Seed 1 False Fragment for a Minor Ordeal, 2 for a Moderate and 3 for a Major, in the same way as real Fragments. A False Fragment looks exactly like a true one and points to a false task or a false Cause.

  • False Fragments never count towards Prepared. If the party thinks an Ordeal is Prepared but holds a False Fragment, the next Omen does not begin it. It reveals the form of the missing true Fragment instead, as normal. That is the tell.
  • Testing a Fragment. Any character may test a Fragment they hold with a Lore or Wits check at the Ordeal’s task BAM!, or by spending 1 Boon Point. On a success, the Narrator says whether it is true. Exposing a False Fragment grants Insight: Minor Advantage on that Ordeal’s decisive check. Insight from several red herrings does not add up.
  • Acting on a False Fragment. The false task can be attempted like any other, with its encounters and its time. Completing it grants no Advantage. When it is done, the deception comes out, and a Setback strikes.
  • False Fragments carry the Taint. A False Fragment shows the Ordeal’s Taint like any true one, so the Taint never exposes a lie on its own. Its tell is that it carries the Taint too neatly, or in the wrong place: frost on a relic in a warm room, grave soil on a witness who never left the village. Describe the tell lightly when the Fragment is found. Sharp players may catch it before they test.
1d6 Red herring Exposed by
1 Scapegoat: an innocent person or family is framed for the Trouble. Anima: their story holds up, and someone wanted them blamed.
2 Lesser monster: a real beast is blamed for the Adversary’s work. Killing it changes nothing. Wits: its tracks, its kills or its hunting grounds do not match.
3 Planted relic: an object left by the Adversary’s servants. Lore: it is too new, too clean, or from the wrong place.
4 Lying witness: paid, frightened or under a charm. Anima: they contradict themselves, or someone else saw them paid.
5 False vision: a dream the Adversary sends. Spiritus: it is too clear, and it asks something of you.
6 Old sin: a real crime in the place’s past, unrelated to this Trouble, that the place wants buried. Lore or Anima: it happened, but long before the Trouble began.

On the Fated Road, each Adversary also has a favourite lie, which it tells in every Ordeal of its Goal. Use it as one of a Moderate or Major Ordeal’s False Fragments (see The Adversary’s favourite lie).

Challenges

Challenges are the personal trials that decide who a character becomes. A character faces one Challenge per band, its tier matching the band, and it can be woven into an Ordeal so the personal and the world-sized stories meet.

At a glance. A Challenge tests one attribute at Average (13), Difficult (16) or Challenging (19). It needs 2, 3 or 4 Fragments. Success Locks the attribute and grants an Identity Mark: a Vow or Knot (Minor), a Vow or Scar (Moderate), or a Scar or Knot (Major). Refusing or failing it brings its Blight. Use the Challenge Generator for its trial, site, Fragments, Omen and Blight.

One per band. Each character may face one Challenge in each band: a Minor Challenge between 1st and 3rd Degree, a Moderate Challenge between 4th and 5th, and a Major Challenge between 6th and 9th. A Challenge does not count towards Degrees; it shapes the character, not the road.

Woven Challenges. A Challenge can ride inside an Ordeal of the same tier:

  • Its site is one of the Ordeal’s sites, or a place inside the footprint.
  • One of its Fragments can be found during a task’s encounter instead of by its own route.
  • Its Omen can be the Ordeal’s Omen, so both begin together. The Challenge is resolved during the Ordeal, before the decisive check.
  • If the trial matches a task (Stand against a foe and Hold a position, say), the Challenge check and the task check can be the same roll, made at the higher of the two BAM!s.

When a Challenge fails inside an Ordeal, its Blight applies, but the Ordeal goes on. When an Ordeal fails, any Challenge woven into it that has not yet been resolved is lost; its Fragments must be found again.

Escape the Bleak Forest. That game has no Degrees, so Challenges use its own optional rule (see Tier): the tier equals the number of Locked attributes + 1.

Ordeal Scale

An Ordeal’s tier sets how much of the map it covers. A Minor Ordeal stays inside one settlement or site, for instance, while a Moderate Ordeal fills a town, a city, or several sites in one stretch of country. A Major Ordeal spreads over a region of several Overland hexes.

Tier Scale Footprint on the map Sites Typical length
Minor A village, hamlet, holdfast, shrine or crossing One Exploration hex (500 m) and the Operational hex (2 km) around it 1 1 to 3 days
Moderate A town or city, or a district of several sites One Overland hex (8 km), or 2 to 4 joined Operational hexes 2 to 3 3 to 7 days
Major A region: a valley, a march, a river’s length, a forest 3 to 5 joined Overland hexes (24 to 40 km across), linked by a road, river or ridge 4, one per task 1 to 3 weeks
Reckoning The heart of the Goal, and every place it touches The Adversary’s seat, plus every site of the Goal’s Major Ordeals 5 or more As long as it takes

Sites. A site is where a task is done: a mill, a gaol, a barrow, a bridge. In a Moderate Ordeal, give each site its own sub-hex. In a Major Ordeal, put each task’s site in a different Overland hex, so the characters must travel between them, and the Danger Die runs between sites as normal.

Where Fragments lie. Every Fragment of an Ordeal is found inside its footprint. In a Moderate or Major Ordeal, each task’s Fragment lies at that task’s site or on the way to it. The Cause Fragment lies where the wrongness is strongest.

Where the Omen falls. The Omen of a Minor Ordeal happens in the place. The Omen of a Moderate Ordeal is seen in any settlement in its Overland hex. The Omen of a Major Ordeal is felt across the whole region. If the characters are outside the footprint when the Omen arrives, they hear of it as a Rumour, and the Ordeal begins when they next enter the footprint.

Choosing the place. An Ordeal from the Goal tables names its place, and the Ordeal Generator rolls one. To set it on your map, put a Minor Ordeal in the nearest settlement of the right kind. Put a Moderate Ordeal in the nearest town or city, or the nearest group of sites that fits. Put a Major Ordeal in the region the characters have not yet crossed.

Ordeal Structure

An Ordeal is a chain of tasks, each guarded by encounters, ending in one decisive check against its Cause. Bigger Ordeals have more tasks, more encounters in each task, and stronger creatures.

Tier Tasks Task BAM! Encounters per task Cause encounter Encounters in all Creatures Decisive BAM! Fragments
Minor 2 Average (13) 1 None About 2 to 3 Common table, Might 0 Difficult (16) 3
Moderate 3 Difficult (16) 2 1 About 7 Uncommon table, up to Tier I Challenging (19) 4
Major 4 Challenging (19) 3 1 About 13 Rare table, up to Tier II Formidable (22) 5
Reckoning 5, plus 1 per Hold Formidable (22) 3 1, the Adversary 16 or more The Adversary, Tier III Inconceivable (25) 6 Threads

These numbers are for a party of 3 or 4; Party Size scales them for 1 to 6 characters. The Reckoning is the last Ordeal of the Fated Road; its own rules are under The Reckoning. The totals count only the encounters inside tasks and at the Cause. Travel between sites, foraging and Fragment hunting add their own Danger Die encounters as normal.

Tasks

Each task has an attribute, a site and a check. The Goal tables, or the Ordeal Generator’s Tasks step, give every task’s attribute and what it asks. Identity Marks apply to tasks whenever their benefits, constraints or complications are relevant. A task’s check uses the attribute named and whichever skill fits the approach the player describes.

Encounters within a task

Before the task check, play out its encounters. An Ordeal from the Goal tables names its task encounters; when you need more, or want variety, roll 1d6 on the table below. Each encounter is a full scene, resolved with the normal encounter, combat and skill rules.

1d6 Encounter What it is
1–2 Adversary A creature or band of the Ordeal’s tier stands in the way. Fight, parley, hide, flee or trick it.
3 Hazard The site itself is dangerous: a collapse, a flood, a fire, a fall. One check at the task’s BAM!; on a failure, take the harm the hazard threatens.
4 Witness Someone knows something. Parley at the task’s BAM!; on a success they give a Fragment for another event or tell you the Cause’s weakness.
5 Temptation An easier way, at a price: Corruption, a broken Vow, a life traded. Accepting it counts the encounter as resolved.
6 Signature On the Fated Road, an encounter from the Goal’s own signature table. Off the road, roll again.

When the task’s encounters are done:

  • All resolved in your favour (won, avoided, talked down or survived without retreat): the task check gains Moderate Advantage.
  • Mixed: no change.
  • All lost or fled: the task check is at Moderate Disadvantage.

The decisive check

When every task has been attempted, the characters confront the Cause. In a Moderate or Major Ordeal, play the Cause encounter first. Then one character, the Lead, makes the decisive check against the Ordeal’s Decisive BAM!, using the attribute and skill that fit how they face the Cause. The others can Support it (see Party Size), and their earlier work shows in the Advantage from completed tasks.

  • Each completed task grants Moderate Advantage on the decisive check.
  • New limit: the Advantage from completed tasks stacks to a maximum of Extreme (3 steps). Without this, a Major Ordeal’s four tasks would drop Formidable (22) all the way to Easy (10).
  • Each failed task leaves its complication in play. Its encounter, hazard or harm is still waiting at the decisive check, and the Narrator may apply Minor Disadvantage for each one.

Success

The Trouble is resolved and the place changes for good. Every character who was the Lead or a Second in a task counts the Ordeal towards their Degrees. An Identity Mark may be deepened, reinterpreted or added when the outcome calls for it. An Ordeal from the Goal tables says what its success brings.

Near miss: success at a cost

If the decisive check fails by 1 or 2, and the die did not show a natural 1, the Ordeal succeeds at a cost. The Trouble is resolved, every character who took a role counts the Ordeal towards their Degrees, and on the Fated Road a Major Ordeal’s Cause Fragment still becomes a Thread. But the Adversary takes its due:

  • on the Fated Road, the Goal’s Adversary gains a Hold; and
  • no Doom is restored.

Spend Boon on the roll first if you mean to: a near miss is judged on the final result. In a general Ordeal off the Fated Road, where there is no Adversary to gain a Hold, the place’s Danger Die rises by 1 for good instead. The Reckoning has no near miss: the age turns, or it does not.

Failure and Blights

Roll 1d6 on the Blight table. Whatever the roll, on the Fated Road the Goal’s Adversary also gains a Hold.

1d6 Blight
1–2 The place is lost. Its Danger Die rises by 1 for good.
3–4 The Cause spreads to a neighbouring place as a new Ordeal one tier higher (a Major Ordeal spreads as a Major Ordeal with one more task).
5–6 The place’s people, or its faction, become Hostile to you.

Setbacks and the Ruin Die

Once an Ordeal’s Omen arrives, the Trouble gets worse until it is stopped. The Ruin die tracks how long the characters have; Setbacks are what go wrong along the way.

The Ruin die

When the Omen arrives, set a d6 to 6. Lower it by 1:

  • at the end of each day of a Minor Ordeal, every 2 days of a Moderate Ordeal, or every 3 days of a Major Ordeal; and
  • each time a Setback strikes.

A task check that succeeds with style (beating its BAM! by 6 or more) raises the Ruin die by 1, to a maximum of 6.

At 0, the Trouble peaks. Roll on the Blight table now: the place suffers it whatever happens next. The decisive check can still be made, at Moderate Disadvantage. Success still ends the Trouble and counts towards Degrees, but restores no Doom.

Setbacks

A Setback strikes when:

  • a task check fails;
  • any task, Support or decisive check rolls a natural 1;
  • the party loses or flees an encounter inside a task; or
  • the Wyrd die calls for one.

Each Setback lowers the Ruin die by 1. Then roll 1d6.

1d6 Setback Effect
1 Delay Lose 1 Watch: the road floods, the witness hides, the door is barred.
2 Losses Lose 1d3 uses of food, water or torches, or 1 Durability from one item.
3 Hunted The Adversary knows you are here. Add one Adversary encounter to your next task.
4 Turned The place’s people turn Wary. Anima tasks are at Minor Disadvantage until a task succeeds.
5 Wounded The character with the lowest Vitality takes a Minor Condition of the Narrator’s choice.
6 The Adversary’s gaze Every character in the footprint makes a Doom save.

A character may spend 1 Boon Point to cancel a Setback as it is rolled. The Ruin die still drops.

Party Size

An Ordeal works for 1 to 6 characters. The number of tasks and Fragments never changes, so every Ordeal works as written, whether it comes from the Goal tables or the generator. What scales is who takes part in each task, how many encounters each task holds, and how many enemies each encounter brings. Every character must take a role in at least one task, or the Ordeal does not count for them.

Roles in a task

Every task has one Lead and any number of Seconds.

  • Lead: makes the task check. One per task.
  • Second: takes part in one of two ways:
    • Face an encounter. The Second is the one who carries one of the task’s encounters: makes its key roll, takes point in the fight, or does the talking. Others may join in as normal.
    • Support the check. The Second describes how they help and makes a check with any attribute and skill that fits, at the task’s BAM!. One successful Support gives the Lead Minor Advantage; two or more give Moderate Advantage. That is the most Supports can give. A natural 1 on a Support check is a Skill Mishap for the supporter (a Magical Misfire for a Spiritus check).

A character can take a role in several tasks. To count the Ordeal towards Degrees, each character must be the Lead or a Second in at least one task. The decisive check works the same way: one Lead, and Seconds who can Support it.

Encounters by party size

The encounter numbers in Ordeal Structure are for a party of 3 or 4. Use this table for other sizes.

Characters Minor Moderate Major Reckoning
1 1 per task 1 per task 2 per task 2 per task
2 1 per task 2 per task 2 per task 3 per task
3–4 1 per task 2 per task 3 per task 3 per task
5–6 2 per task 3 per task 4 per task 4 per task

When the table adds encounters, roll them on the encounter table in Ordeal Structure, or, on the Fated Road, use the Goal’s signature encounters.

Enemies by party size

The Number listed for a creature in the Creature Compendium is for a party of 3 or 4.

Characters Number appearing Cause encounter
1 Half, rounded up The Cause alone, without its guards
2 Half, rounded up The Cause and one guard
3–4 As listed As written
5–6 Half again, rounded up As written, plus one more creature of the Ordeal’s tier

Never scale a single named creature (a champion, a lieutenant, the Adversary) up or down. Scale the creatures around it.

How it plays at each size

  • 1 character (solo): the character is the Lead of every task. There are no other characters to act as Seconds, but there are fewer encounters and fewer enemies, and the character’s Companions can Support (see Companions as Seconds). A solo Major Ordeal is 8 encounters and the Cause.
  • 2 characters: they share the tasks between them, Leading some and Supporting others. A Minor Ordeal gives each one task to Lead.
  • 3 or 4 characters: the standard. In a Minor Ordeal, at least one character will be a Second only; give them an encounter to carry.
  • 5 or 6 characters: more encounters than characters, so everyone carries something. The Narrator should make sure no one sits out a whole task: a Second who has no encounter to face Supports the check instead.

Worked out, a party of 6 in a Minor Ordeal has 2 tasks with 2 encounters each: 2 Leads and 4 encounters to carry, one role each.

Companions as Seconds

A character’s Companions, their Devoted Followers and Soul Bound animals, can stand beside them in an Ordeal. A Companion can act as a Second in a task by Supporting its check. It cannot Lead a task or the decisive check, and it never counts an Ordeal towards Degrees.

  • Companion Support. The Companion makes a check at the task’s BAM!, adding a bonus set by the Focus that granted it: +2 at Competent, +4 at Accomplished and +6 at Adept. The Narrator may allow a hireling or other ally at +2. A success gives the Lead Minor Advantage. A natural 1 is a Skill Mishap for the Companion.
  • Solo play. A solo character’s Companions may each Support a task, and one Companion may carry one encounter in each Ordeal in the character’s place.

Keeping the characters at the centre. In a party, these limits keep Companions from taking over:

  1. The Support cap still applies. All Supports together give at most Moderate Advantage.
  2. A Companion adds; it does not complete. A Companion’s Support only ever counts as Minor Advantage. Moderate Advantage needs at least two successful Supports from characters.
  3. One Companion per player in each task.
  4. Characters carry the encounters. In a party, a Companion never carries an encounter; a character does.
Companions and the Adversary (Narrator options). A Companion who stands in an Ordeal is part of it, and the Adversary knows it. These are options for the Narrator, or results for a solo player to apply on a Setback or a Wyrd die:
  • The Setback falls on the Companion. When a task a Companion Supported fails, its Setback may strike the Companion instead: wounded, captured, lost or scattered. The Ruin die still drops.
  • The lie takes root. Companions are the first to believe the Goal’s favourite lie, and the easiest mark for a Temptation encounter. A Companion who believes it may bring the party a False Fragment in good faith.
  • A Companion becomes a Hold. When an Ordeal fails, the Adversary’s new Hold may be a Companion turned, corrupted or taken. Breaking that Hold means facing them.
  • Faith falters. While a character’s Doom is Failing (1–3), their Companions make Support checks at Minor Disadvantage.
  • The price of the Reckoning. One of the Reckoning’s tasks may demand a sacrifice. A Companion who has walked the road since the Minor band gives it weight.

Characters who miss an Ordeal

A character who was absent, or who took no role, does not count the Ordeal. To keep a party together, the Narrator may allow catching up: an absent character’s next completed Ordeal counts as two Ordeals of their band. Catching up can never raise a character more than one Degree from one Ordeal, never above the party’s highest Degree, and never through a gate.

Travel, Terrain and the Danger Die

Ordeals happen on the map, so every travel rule still applies inside an Ordeal’s footprint, and the Trouble makes the land worse. Getting from one site to the next is travel. Terrain and weather slow you down and make tasks harder, and the Danger Die keeps rolling.

Moving between sites

Travel between sites uses trekking speed, the terrain and weather multipliers, and Forced March, all as normal. In a Moderate Ordeal each site is its own sub-hex, and in a Major Ordeal each is its own Overland hex, so a Major Ordeal is also a journey.

The Danger Die inside the footprint

Roll the Danger Die on entering each new sub-hex, as normal, with these changes while an Ordeal is Prepared or under way:

  • +1 die for the Trouble, and +1 more in the hex of a Major Ordeal’s Cause. The pool still never goes above 4d6.
  • When the sum gives an encounter, roll 1d6. On 1–3, use the region’s normal table of that rarity. On 4–6, the encounter belongs to the Ordeal: roll on the Ordeal encounter table in Ordeal Structure, with creatures of the Ordeal’s tier.
  • When the Wyrd die shows 1, roll 1d6 instead of a Special Event. On 1–2, a Setback strikes. On 3–4, you meet a Witness, or find a Fragment for another event. On 5–6, the Trouble flares: roll for the Omen now (if the Ordeal is Prepared).
  • Rest inside the footprint is never safe. Resting without a guard adds a die as normal, and no Full Rest is possible at the Cause’s site.

Terrain at the site

A task done outdoors is shaped by the ground it is done on. Tasks done indoors, underground or in a settlement ignore this table.

Terrain at the site Effect on tasks there
Speed ×1 (road, grassland, light forest) None.
Speed ×¾ (rolling hills, scrub, coast, desert, forest) Corpus tasks at Minor Disadvantage.
Speed ×½ (steep hills, tundra, swamp, ruins, dense forest) Corpus tasks at Moderate Disadvantage. Hazard encounters deal 1 more harm. Stealth tasks at Minor Advantage.
Speed ×¼ (mountains) Corpus tasks at Major Disadvantage. Hazard encounters deal 1d3 more harm.
Dead forest Spiritus tasks at Minor Disadvantage. Nothing can be foraged for the task.
Cursed or blighted forest Spiritus tasks at Moderate Disadvantage, and Doom saves there are one step harder.

Weather on the day

Roll the weather (2d6) at the start of each Watch as normal. It applies to every task done outdoors that Watch, on top of its effect on travel.

2d6 Weather Effect on tasks outdoors
2 Downpour Corpus and sight-based tasks at Moderate Disadvantage. No task that needs fire.
3–4 Fog Sight-based and tracking tasks at Moderate Disadvantage. Stealth tasks at Moderate Advantage.
5–8 Clear None.
9 Snow Tracking tasks at Major Advantage. Corpus tasks at Minor Disadvantage.
10–11 Rain Ranged and fire-based tasks at Minor Disadvantage.
12 High Winds Climbing tasks at Moderate Disadvantage; ranged tasks beyond Near at Major Disadvantage. No task that needs fire.

The characters may wait for better weather. Waiting costs time, and time is what the Ruin die measures.
