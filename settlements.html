Settlements, Trade, & Hirelings

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

Settlements

Settlements are where the characters rest, trade, heal and hear Rumours. A settlement that is not inside an active Ordeal’s footprint, not lost to a Blight, and not Hostile to the characters is a safe place: a Full Rest there restores 1 Doom (see Rest and Recovery). Inside a safe place, roll no Danger Die.

Size Looks like What can be bought Services Hirelings available
Hamlet A few farms Some Common items (4+ on 1d6) Food, water, a barn to sleep in 1d3 labourers
Village A church, a smith, an alehouse Common items Lodging, a healer of sorts, repairs to simple gear 1d6 labourers or porters
Market town A market, a guild, a lord’s hall Common items; Uncommon on 4+ on 1d6 All lodging and repairs, healers, scribes, a few trainers 2d6 of any kind
City Walls, a cathedral, many guilds Common and Uncommon items; Rare on 5+ on 1d6 Everything, including rare trainers and buyers for Rare goods As many as you can pay

To find something that is not always there, roll the d6 shown when the characters first look for it in a settlement. On a failure it is not to be had there this visit.

Generating a Settlement

In Solo and Co-Op play, or when a Narrator needs a place at once, roll up a settlement the first time the characters arrive. Record it: it stays the same on every visit, apart from what the characters change.

  1. Size. Take it from the map. If the map does not say, roll 1d6: 1–2 hamlet, 3–4 village, 5 market town, 6 city.
  2. Name. Build a name as for a person (People on the Fly) and add a d6 ending: 1 -ford, 2 -by, 3 -wick, 4 -ham, 5 -stead, 6 -mere. Bran with -ford makes Branford.
  3. Mood. Roll 1d6 on the Mood table.
  4. Who rules. Roll 1d6: 1 An elder or headman; 2 a priest or abbot; 3 the lord’s steward; 4 a guild council; 5 a knight and their household; 6 no one: rival families feud. Make the ruler with People on the Fly.
  5. Factions. A hamlet has none beyond its ruler, a village 1, a market town 2 and a city 3. Roll each on the Factions table.
  6. Places. Roll the number of places for its size on the Places table, rerolling repeats. Encounters and Ordeal sites happen there.

Mood

1d6 Mood Effect
1 Prosperous The find roll for Uncommon goods, and for Rare goods in a city, needs one less than usual.
2 Fearful Gates and doors shut at dusk. −1 to strangers’ disposition rolls after dark.
3 Devout The church holds sway. Healers charge half. Working magic in the open costs 1 Standing.
4 Lawless +1 on the Settlement Die. A fence is always to be found.
5 Grieving Sickness or loss has struck. Food costs double, and people talk: roll one extra Rumour.
6 Hiding something One of its factions is a Court cell, whatever the faction roll says.

Factions

Roll 1d6 for each faction, and 1d6 for what it wants. Make its leader with People on the Fly. Every faction starts at Standing 0. Reroll a second Court cell.

1d6 Faction Cares about Wants (1d6)
1 The lord’s household Order, taxes and its own standing 1: Power over the others
2 The church Faith kept, and heresy rooted out 2: Coin
3 A guild Trade, prices and its monopoly 3: Safety from the Forest
4 The watch or the reeve’s men Quiet streets and no trouble from strangers 4: Revenge on another faction
5 Outlaws or thieves Coin, and the watch kept away 5: To bury a sin
6 A Court cell To serve the Goal’s Adversary unseen. Its members use the Court Agent stat block, and its leader is a lieutenant. 6: To drive strangers out

A Court cell is secret. Its members look like ordinary townsfolk until a Fragment, a Taint or a careless word gives them away. Any Rumour its members spread is their Goal’s favourite lie.

Settlement Encounters

A safe place rolls no Danger Die, but it is not empty. For each Watch the characters spend in a settlement, roll the Settlement Die instead: 1d6, adding:

  • +1 at night;
  • +1 in a market town, or +2 in a city;
  • +1 if the settlement is Lawless;
  • +1 if the characters’ Standing there is −1, or +2 if it is −2 or worse.
Roll Result What happens
1–3 Quiet The Watch passes.
4–5 Locals Someone crosses the characters’ path: roll on People on the Fly, then their disposition.
6–7 A faction A member of one of the settlement’s factions takes an interest. Roll which, and what it wants of the characters.
8 An official The watch, a tax-gatherer or a priest asks questions. Roll 1d6: 1–3 routine; 4–5 a fee of 1d6s; 6 an accusation (Trouble 3).
9–10 Trouble Roll on the Trouble table.
11+ The Adversary’s hand Roll on the Trouble table. Whatever it is, a Court Agent is behind it.

A settlement that is not a safe place, because it is inside an active footprint, lost to a Blight or Hostile to the characters, rolls the Danger Die as usual.

Trouble

1d6 Trouble
1 A thief tries a pocket or a pack. Make a Wits save, Average (13), or lose one item.
2 A brawl, or a crowd turns ugly at strangers: 2d6 Fooled Folk, Wary.
3 An accusation: someone blames the characters for a theft, a death or a sickness. Standing falls by 1 unless they clear their names.
4 A fire, a flood or a collapse. Helping gains 1 Standing.
5 Thugs move against the characters: a Court Agent with 1d3 Retainers, or 1d6 Retainers paid by a faction.
6 Something gets in from outside. Roll on the region’s Uncommon encounter table; after dark, it is in the streets.

Reactions in Settlements

Roll disposition on 2d6 as usual for anyone the characters meet, and add:

Modifier When
+ Standing With that settlement or faction (see Standing below).
+1 The characters share the person’s faith, home or trade.
+1 The characters were introduced, have paid, or have helped them.
−1 The characters are armed and bloody, or arrive after dark in a Fearful place.
−1 Strangers in a hamlet.
−2 They saw the characters work magic in a Devout place, or they belong to a Court cell and suspect what the characters are.

Fooled Folk start no better than Wary, whatever the roll, until they are shown a true Fragment.

Shops and Trainers

Merchants

When the characters find someone selling what they want (see the find roll under Settlements), roll 1d6 once for that merchant and record it.

1d6 Merchant Effect
1 Honest Fair prices.
2 Greedy Prices a quarter higher. Haggling is at Minor Advantage.
3 Desperate Prices a quarter lower, but only one of each item.
4 Suspicious Deals only at Standing +1 or better, or after a Guile check, Average (13).
5 A fence Buys anything, Rare goods included, at a third of its cost, and asks no questions.
6 Tainted Fair prices, but the owner serves the Adversary. Their goods carry the Goal’s signature Taint.

Trainers

A Training Ordeal still needs its leads, and its trainer still lives where the Advancement rules say. A settlement can supply one of those leads: the first time the characters ask after a trainer for a skill or Focus there, roll 1d6. On the number shown, someone there knows of one, and that is one lead.

Size Minor training Moderate training
Hamlet — —
Village 6 (Minor only) —
Market town 5+ —
City 4+ 6

Major training is never found in a settlement: its trainer is a legend at the edge of the map.

Arriving at a Settlement

  1. Generate it if it is new (see Generating a Settlement).
  2. Note the characters’ Standing with the settlement and its factions.
  3. Choose Watch actions for each character: Trade, Seek services, Gather rumours, Hire, Rest.
  4. Roll the Settlement Die for each Watch, and resolve what it brings.
  5. Meet people with People on the Fly and the reaction modifiers above.
  6. Leave when the characters are ready, and record what has changed.

In a Settlement

Time in a settlement still passes in Watches, and each of these is a Watch action (see Strategic turns):

  • Trade: buy and sell. Each character can make one deal for each kind of goods.
  • Seek services: a healer, smith, scribe, sage or trainer.
  • Gather rumours: drink, listen and ask. Make a Guile check, Average (13), adding the settlement’s Standing. On a success, roll on the Rumours table once in a hamlet or village, twice in a market town and three times in a city, with one more if you Succeed with style. On a natural 1 you ask the wrong person, and someone hears that you are asking. A Rumour from a Court cell is its Goal’s favourite lie.
  • Hire: find and hire hirelings (see Hirelings).
  • Rest: as usual, in a bed if you pay for one.

Buying and Selling

  • Selling: a buyer pays half an item’s cost, less one tenth for each point of Durability it has lost. Loot coins are taken at face value.
  • Rare goods sell only in a market town or a city, and only if a buyer can be found (the same d6 roll as buying).
  • Haggling: make a Guile check, Average (13). On a success the price moves a quarter in your favour; if you Succeed with style, by half. On a natural 1 the seller is insulted and will not deal with you this visit.
  • Standing changes prices too (see Standing).

Standing

Standing measures how a settlement or a faction, such as a guild, a lord’s household or one of the Adversary’s Courts, regards the characters. It runs from −3 to +3, and every party starts at 0 with everyone. Record it for each place and faction that matters.

Standing Name Effect
−3 Hated The characters are driven out or attacked on sight. No one trades with them.
−2 Unwelcome −2 to disposition rolls; prices are doubled, when anyone will sell at all.
−1 Distrusted −1 to disposition rolls.
0 Strangers No change.
+1 Known +1 to disposition rolls.
+2 Trusted +2 to disposition rolls; lodging is free and prices are a quarter lower.
+3 Honoured As Trusted, and the place will take real risks for the characters.
  • Disposition: add Standing to the 2d6 disposition roll for anyone from that place or faction (see disposition).
  • Gaining Standing: +1 for completing a Challenge or Minor Ordeal for the place, +2 for a Moderate Ordeal, +3 for a Major Ordeal. +1 for a public kindness or a debt honoured.
  • Losing Standing: −1 for a theft, a broken promise or harming the fooled. −2 for a killing. When a Blight makes a faction Hostile, its Standing with the characters falls to −2.
  • The Courts start at 0 and can be dealt with like any faction, until they learn what the characters are. When the characters complete an Ordeal against a Court, its Standing falls to −3.

Hirelings

Hirelings are paid help: porters, torchbearers, guides and swords. They are not Devoted Followers: they work for coin, not loyalty, and they do not Recurse.

Hireling Wage per day Does Stats
Labourer or porter 1s Carries 6 slots; will not fight Fooled Folk
Torchbearer 1s Carries 3 slots and the light Fooled Folk
Guide 3s Moderate Advantage on navigation checks in their home region Fooled Folk
Man-at-arms 5s Fights; carries 3 slots Retainer
Specialist: healer, scribe, sage, smith 1g Uses one skill at Skilled (+2) Fooled Folk, with that skill
  • Hiring: take the Hire action in a settlement that has them, and make a Guile check, Average (13), adding the settlement’s Standing. On a success, 1d3 will sign on; on a failure, none this visit. Pay a week in advance, or a day if they are Trusting.
  • Loyalty: a hireling makes a morale save, Anima against Average (13), when they first see something supernatural, when a companion dies, when their pay is late, and whenever a group would check morale. On a failure they flee or quit. Each week of good treatment gives them +1 on these saves, to a maximum of +3.
  • In Ordeals: a hireling can help a task check as an ally at +2 if the Narrator allows (see Ordeal Structure), but cannot be the Lead or a Second.
  • Danger and death: hirelings take Vitality damage and Conditions as characters do. A hireling who dies stays dead; their kin will want to know how, and the settlement’s Standing may fall.

Time Between Ordeals

Time outside an Ordeal costs no Ruin (see Time outside an Ordeal), so the road leaves room for the characters to recover and prepare. Between Ordeals, the characters can:

  • take a Full Rest in a safe place to restore Doom and heal attributes;
  • see a healer about Conditions;
  • sell loot and buy gear, components and mounts;
  • repair, craft and copy workings (see Long work);
  • seek a trainer for a Training Ordeal;
  • gather Rumours and follow up on Fragments;
  • raise their Standing, and hire help for the road ahead.

Three things still run: the Danger Die outside safe places, the Omen roll for any Prepared event inside its footprint, and the Ruin die of any Ordeal that has already begun, which cannot be paused (see When time runs out). A Training Ordeal’s end also rolls every Prepared Omen.
