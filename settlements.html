Settlements, Trade, & Hirelings

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

Settlements

Settlements are where the characters rest, trade, heal and hear Rumours. A settlement that is not inside an active Ordeal’s footprint, not lost to a Blight, and not Hostile to the characters is a safe place: a Full Rest there restores 1 Doom (see Rest and Recovery). Inside a safe place, roll no Danger Die.

To find something that is not always there, roll the d6 shown when the characters first look for it in a settlement. On a failure it is not to be had there this visit.

In a Settlement

Time in a settlement still passes in Watches, and each of these is a Watch action (see Strategic turns):

  • Trade: buy and sell. Each character can make one deal for each kind of goods.
  • Seek services: a healer, smith, scribe, sage or trainer.
  • Gather rumours: drink, listen and ask. Make a Guile check, Average (13): on a success roll once on the Rumours table, twice if you Succeed with style. On a natural 1 you ask the wrong person, and someone hears that you are asking.
  • Hire: find and hire hirelings (see Hirelings).
  • Rest: as usual, in a bed if you pay for one.

Prices

Coins are copper (c), silver (s) and gold (g): 10c make 1s, and 10s make 1g. Weapons and armour are priced on the Equipment page. Prices here are for the Bleak Forest and change with scarcity: double them in a place under a Blight, or far from any road.

Buying and Selling

  • Selling: a buyer pays half an item’s cost, less one tenth for each point of Durability it has lost. Loot coins are taken at face value.
  • Rare goods sell only in a market town or a city, and only if a buyer can be found (the same d6 roll as buying).
  • Haggling: make a Guile check, Average (13). On a success the price moves a quarter in your favour; if you Succeed with style, by half. On a natural 1 the seller is insulted and will not deal with you this visit.
  • Standing changes prices too (see Standing).

Standing

Standing measures how a settlement or a faction, such as a guild, a lord’s household or one of the Adversary’s Courts, regards the characters. It runs from −3 to +3, and every party starts at 0 with everyone. Record it for each place and faction that matters.

Standing Name Effect
−3 Hated The characters are driven out or attacked on sight. No one trades with them.
−2 Unwelcome −2 to disposition rolls; prices are doubled, when anyone will sell at all.
−1 Distrusted −1 to disposition rolls.
0 Strangers No change.
+1 Known +1 to disposition rolls.
+2 Trusted +2 to disposition rolls; lodging is free and prices are a quarter lower.
+3 Honoured As Trusted, and the place will take real risks for the characters.
  • Disposition: add Standing to the 2d6 disposition roll for anyone from that place or faction (see disposition).
  • Gaining Standing: +1 for completing a Challenge or Minor Ordeal for the place, +2 for a Moderate Ordeal, +3 for a Major Ordeal. +1 for a public kindness or a debt honoured.
  • Losing Standing: −1 for a theft, a broken promise or harming the fooled. −2 for a killing. When a Blight makes a faction Hostile, its Standing with the characters falls to −2.
  • The Courts start at 0 and can be dealt with like any faction, until they learn what the characters are. When the characters complete an Ordeal against a Court, its Standing falls to −3.

Hirelings

Hirelings are paid help: porters, torchbearers, guides and swords. They are not Devoted Followers: they work for coin, not loyalty, and they do not Recurse.

Hireling Wage per day Does Stats
Labourer or porter 1s Carries 6 slots; will not fight Fooled Folk
Torchbearer 1s Carries 3 slots and the light Fooled Folk
Guide 3s Moderate Advantage on navigation checks in their home region Fooled Folk
Man-at-arms 5s Fights; carries 3 slots Retainer
Specialist: healer, scribe, sage, smith 1g Uses one skill at Skilled (+2) Fooled Folk, with that skill
  • Hiring: take the Hire action in a settlement that has them, and make a Guile check, Average (13), adding the settlement’s Standing. On a success, 1d3 will sign on; on a failure, none this visit. Pay a week in advance, or a day if they are Trusting.
  • Loyalty: a hireling makes a morale save, Anima against Average (13), when they first see something supernatural, when a companion dies, when their pay is late, and whenever a group would check morale. On a failure they flee or quit. Each week of good treatment gives them +1 on these saves, to a maximum of +3.
  • In Ordeals: a hireling can help a task check as an ally at +2 if the Narrator allows (see Ordeal Structure), but cannot be the Lead or a Second.
  • Danger and death: hirelings take Vitality damage and Conditions as characters do. A hireling who dies stays dead; their kin will want to know how, and the settlement’s Standing may fall.

Time Between Ordeals

Time outside an Ordeal costs no Ruin (see Time outside an Ordeal), so the road leaves room for the characters to recover and prepare. Between Ordeals, the characters can:

  • take a Full Rest in a safe place to restore Doom and heal attributes;
  • see a healer about Conditions;
  • sell loot and buy gear, components and mounts;
  • repair, craft and copy workings (see Long work);
  • seek a trainer for a Training Ordeal;
  • gather Rumours and follow up on Fragments;
  • raise their Standing, and hire help for the road ahead.

Two things still run: the Danger Die outside safe places, and the Omen roll for any Prepared event inside its footprint. A Training Ordeal’s end also rolls every Prepared Omen.
