Bleak Forest

Tabletop RPG System

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

The Forest is infected and its rot is spreading. You will stop it. There is no other option. In a bleak world where your trials are carved in pain, darkness, and blood, the cycle must be completed for the world to be reborn. The path is brutal and your place in its rebirth is yours to define. Will you achieve greatness, or will you fall short of glory before you are remade?

The Bleak Forest is a tabletop roleplaying game system set in a grim world of dark fantasy. You are navigating an environment beset by challenges at every turn. Yeah, you’ve heard it all before – a dark scourge and a grim world where peril awaits around every corner in a doom-haunted world. Where life is short and you must manage resources and master your abilities to stave off the pain and misery around you. While that certainly is an element in this game (and a big part of the flavour), there is something more on offer here. The Bleak Forest game system is a game of ominous positivity: Your world is failing and you will see it complete its cycle – there simply is no other choice. As the world falls into darkness, disease, and despair, it will eventually be reborn. Your part in that rebirth is inescapable, but the hell you must endure to arrive at the other side is defined by your actions. Inspired by mythic cycles of calamity and rebirth such as the Norse tales of Ragnarok and Fimbulvetr and the Hindu and Buddhist cosmic cycle of Samsara; Beyond the Bleak Forest is set at the last stages of the cycle before rebirth. You are part of that cosmic cycle, though powerful forces work against you, preventing the conclusion of the cycle, drawing out the anguish of a world in transition.

The basic game engine mechanic is simple: roll over a target number, apply modifiers, and determine result. Pretty standard stuff. However, one of the driving mechanical design goals is to create a game that lends itself to different modes of play: Co-Op, GM-led, and Solo. To do that, it needed to be simple enough that there would not be endless lookups and modifier balancing, all the while providing structure and direction necessary for guided play through solo or Co-Op modes, yet be flexible enough to provide free-form GM-guided play and still feel satisfying.

The core rules comprise a common set of mechanics across Bleak Forest Engine Games. This includes:

  • Character creation
  • Core Mechanics
  • Skills, abilities, and magic
  • Basics of gameplay

The core rules engine provides the mechanics common across the Bleak Forest system and gives you a solid foundation for building your own settings and adventures (and even games) on top of them. Some elements—such as character advancement, skills, and magic can and will vary between different Bleak Forest implementations, so the core rules provide simple modular options you can adapt to suit your needs. Below are some of the modular games built upon the core rules engine:

  • Escape the Bleak Forest is a solo character funnel that gamifies character creation. Through cycles of death and return, your character is refined until their true nature emerges and they finally escape the Forest.
  • Beyond the Bleak Forest lies a dying world, haunted and collapsing toward its inevitable renewal. The Abrikoth (six Avatars of Corruption) fight to stop that rebirth and preserve their dominion over the damned. As you carry your character forward from Escape the Bleak Forest, your abilities sharpen, and your fate takes shape. This chapter can be played solo, cooperatively, or with a GM guiding the descent and rebirth.

Designer Note: These modular systems are optional. They’re simply one way to express the core mechanics and add a bit of flavour to the world. You’ll also see subsystems for durability, availability, consumables, and more as part of the core rules engine. I added these because I wanted the game to feel fragile, doomed, and consequential, where even the small choices carry weight. Use whatever serves your table and leave the rest. Gaming should be fun and played as suits your desires, not as a checklist that must be rigidly adhered to. That said, THERE IS NO WRONG WAY TO DO THIS.

And with that, welcome to the core rules for the Bleak Forest TTRPG Game Engine. It’s a living document, so things may shift as the design grows. Have fun and happy gaming!

The Core Mechanic

Almost everything in Bleak Forest resolves the same way: roll a d20, add the relevant attribute modifier and any skill bonus, and try to meet or beat the BAM! (Basic Activity Modifier). Whenever the task requires some skill, focus, or there is an element of risk and chance of failure, then make a BAM! Check. The higher the target score, the more difficult the task.

BAM! Easy Average Difficult Challenging Formidable Inconcievable
Target Score 10 13 16 19 22 25

A natural 1 is a Fumble and always fails (the worst possible outcome). A natural 20 on an attack or action is a Critical Success and always succeeds (except in situations where there is a Might Tier applied – refer to Attributes Section for more on Might).
Succeeding with Style means you have scored 6 over the BAM! target score. This means you have achieved the optimal success for the task (i.e. you find exactly what you were looking for during a foraging check or you score the maximum damge on an attack, etc.).

Saves work the same way: roll d20 + relevant attribute modifier vs. the stated BAM! to avoid something bad.

Checks and Saves

Use a Check when your character tries something difficult or risky, such as climbing a wall, casting a spell, or attacking. Make a Save when your character tries to avoid or resist something harmful, such as poison, a spell, or extreme hardship.

There are two types of checks:

  • Activity Check: Roll a d20. Add the Attribute and Skill bonuses that fit the task. If the total meets or beats the BAM! target number, you succeed.
  • Opposed Check: Use this when characters compete directly. Each character rolls a d20 and adds the right bonuses. Each must meet their own BAM! target. Of those who succeed, the highest total wins. Different characters may have different BAM! targets if one has an advantage or disadvantage.

Rolling a 1 or 20 on a check: A natural 1 is always a fumble. A natural 20 is usually a critical success, but very powerful creatures can reduce its effect. Against Might 4–7, an attack still hits and the creature keeps its automatic bonus damage. Against Might 8+, the attack hits but deals only half damage. See Might Tiers for full details.

Saves: Make a save when your character tries to avoid or resist something harmful, such as poison, a spell, or extreme hardship. Roll a d20 and add the relevant Attribute modifier. If the total meets or beats the BAM! target, you succeed. A save is Easy (10) unless the rule gives a different BAM!.

Rolling a 1 or 20 on a save: A natural 1 is always a fumble. Against a normal threat with Might 0, a natural 20 always succeeds. Against stronger threats, a natural 20 may have a reduced effect. See Might Tiers and Effects for details.

Advantage and Disadvantage

Advantage makes a roll easier. Disadvantage makes it harder. Minor Advantage or Disadvantage adds or subtracts 1 from the roll. Stronger effects move the BAM! target: Moderate moves it 1 step (3 points), Major moves it 2 steps (6 points), and Extreme moves it 3 steps (9 points). If more than one effect applies, see Advantage/Disadvantage in the Modifiers section of Playing the Game.

Tier With Advantage With Disdavantage
Minor Add 1 to the roll Subtract 1 from the roll
Moderate Move BAM! 1 step easier Move BAM! 1 step harder
Major Move BAM! 2 steps easier Move BAM! 2 steps harder
Extreme Move BAM! 3 steps easier Move BAM! 3 steps harder

When Advantage or Disadvantage are applied, each tier has a maximum shift as follows:

  • Moderate: Cannot adjust the BAM! Lower than Easy or higher than Challenging
  • Major:Cannot adjust the BAM! Lower than Easy or higher than Formidable
  • ExtremeCannot adjust the BAM! Lower than Easy or higher than Inconceivable

Stacking Advantages and Disadvantages:

When you encounter multiple advantages or disadvantages of the same type in a single situation, these modifiers DO NOT accumulate. Instead, only the highest single advantage or disadvantage applies for that situation. For example, if both a Moderate and a Major advantage are present for cover, only the Major advantage is used to determine the outcome.

If you are in a situation where different advantages or disadvantages apply, then they do stack. For instance, if you have a Moderate disadvantage to attack for cover and a Major disadvantage due to a being blinded, your effective disadvantage to attack your target is Extreme Disadvantage.

Additionally, advantages and disadvantages counterbalance each other. If a situation presents both an advantage and a disadvantage, they offset accordingly. For instance, if you have a Major advantage but also face a Moderate disadvantage, the net result is a Moderate advantage.

In no case can you gain an Advantage or Disadvantage higher than Inconceivable (25) or lower than Easy (10). Sometimes, rather than directly applying an advantage or disadvantage to yourself; the situation may call for applying an advantage or disadvantage to your opponent.
