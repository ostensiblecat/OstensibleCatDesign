Quick Start

If you have never played a Bleak Forest Engine game before, read this section first. It tells you what you need to know to get started creating your character and where to go to get more details for things like skills, spells, and playing the game.

What You Need:

  • A set of at least six six sided dice (d6) and one twenty-sided die (d20)
  • Pencil and paper (or a printed character sheet)
  • These rules

Create Your Character in 4 Steps:

Character creation involves determining the statistics, skills, and starting basic equipment for the character. The character creation process is as follows:

  1. Attribute Generation: Roll 3d6 for each stat in order, do not change the scores or alter the order (after all, this is the core conceit of the game). If your starting bonuses total zero or lower, you may roll again.
  2. Vitality Calculation: Calculate your starting Vitality score by adding up your attribute bonuses and adding them (or subtracting) the result from a base of 12.
  3. Skills and Focus (Option 1): Roll one d6 on each of the three Attribute Skill tables (Corpus, Anima, Spiritus) to determine your starting skill in each area. These three skills are granted free at Apprentice (+1) rank. Next roll for a focus for each skill granted at Competent Rank.
  4. Skills and Focus (Option 2): Alternative method: You have 9 skill points to distribute, with each rank of a skill or focus costing 1 point. You can only place one point into a skill or a Focus at starting character creation. Maximum starting rank is Expert (+3).
  5. Determine starting gear

I. Character Attributes

Characters are tracked using three primary attributes that quantify their physical and mental prowess.

Attribute Description Common Check
Corpus Corpus represents your physical body. It affects how well you can lift things, climb, dodge, and how well you endure hardship.
Anima Anima represents your mind. It reflects your emotions, determination, and how you interact with others. Anima helps with tasks like convincing people, spotting danger, or noticing hidden things.
Spiritus Spiritus represents your inner spirit or essence. It’s important for things like casting spells, empowering sigils, and making deals with magical beings.

Character attributes follow a standard range from 3-18 for human characters, with 18 representing the highest human potential. Attribute descriptors such as Mediocre or Excellent can be useful in describing the quantification of the particular attribute, but they are not necessary for gameplay.

Attribute values above 18 apply only to non-player creatures, environments, and Challenges. They represent superhuman, environmental, or mythic forces such as monsters, ancient spirits, cursed locations, or existential ordeals.

Might

Might values reflect a level of ability that is beyond human capabilities. These are generally powerful supernatural beings, harsh environments, or otherwise overwhelming challenges that provide a threat level beyond the human scale. Might is not simply a bonus added to dice rolls; it is a tier of dominance that reshapes what is possible when facing the creature. Might is divided into three tiers, each with effects that apply to minimum BAM! ratings, Vitality damage bonus, and impacts to saves against the creature’s attacks and abilities.

The benefits of Might tiers ONLY apply to stats that have a might tier assigned – higher level Might benefits do not transfer down to a creature’s non-elevated stats.

Might Tiers and Effects

Consult the tier below based on the creature’s Might score.

  • Tier 0: at this level, there are no might effects, but this is a useful categorization for determining Conjuration effects across entities of varying power levels. (see Conjuration under Paths Arcane).
  • Tier I - Imposing (Might 1-3): The creature is significantly beyond human capacity but can still be overcome by skilled or lucky characters.
    • BAM! Minimum Vitality Damage Bonus Save Modifiers
    Difficult (16) 1-4 As per normal rules
  • Tier II - Dread (Might 4–7): The creature is a fundamental threat that warrants extreme caution.
    • BAM! Minimum Vitality Damage Bonus Save Modifiers
    Challenging (19) 5-7 A natural 20 on a save produces a partial success only: you avoid the worst outcome but suffers a Minor condition regardless.
  • Tier III - Mythic (Might 8+): The creature exists at a scale that defies mortal limits.
    • BAM! Minimum Vitality Damage Bonus Save Modifiers
    Formidable (22) 8+ A natural 20 is a failure if it is not modified; though if your modified result indicates success, you still incur half damage or the minimum effect
    In addition, the following apply at Might Tier III:
    •  When you declare a defensive or avoidance effect against this creature (such as Defensive Manoeuvres, Absorb Blow with a shield, or Evasion), the effect is used up immediately, whether it works or not.
    •  If the creature is reduced to 0 Vitality by a single hit, it instead drops to 1 Vitality and takes a Severe condition — it cannot be slain outright without a specific narrative or magical condition being met first.

Example in action: A Frost Giant has a Corpus score of 24, making its Might of 6. This places it in Tier II (Dread). If a character tries to resist being knocked prone by the Giant's club with a Corpus save, the required BAM! check is at Challenging (19). When the Giant hits, it inflicts its standard 2d6+3 damage plus an additional 6 automatic bonus Vitality damage (based on its Might score of 6) and forces a save against being knocked prone. Should the character roll a natural 20 on their saving throw, they manage to stay upright but still suffer a Minor (Bloodied) condition from the resulting shockwave.

Assigning Might to Encounters and Challenges:

Environmental threats usually don’t have stats or a BAM! rating. If they do list a BAM!, use that to pick a Might score. If they don’t, use survivability as your benchmark:

  • Might 1–3: Harsh but survivable conditions Equivalent Difficulty: 16 (Difficult) Examples: severe cold, dangerous terrain, smoke-filled rooms A normal person could survive with effort.
  • Might 4–7: Lethal conditions Equivalent Difficulty: 19 (Challenging) Examples: raging fires, collapsing caverns, toxic atmospheres A normal person would need extraordinary luck or special training to survive.
  • Might 8+: Superhuman-level forces Equivalent Difficulty: 22 (Formidable) Examples: volcanic eruptions, crushing pressure, magical storms Survival is impossible without superhuman abilities.
    • Quick rule of thumb:
    • Might 1–3: Inconvenient, risky, but not dangerous.
    • Might 4–7: Requires effort, skill, or preparation.
    • Might 8+: survival is impossible without superhuman aid.
    Assigning Might to Challenges. Challenges use a simpler scale:
    • Might 0 (Minor Challenge): Inconvenient, risky, but not dangerous.
    • Might 1–3 (Moderate Challenge): Requires effort, skill, or preparation.
    • Might 4–7 (Major Challenge): Beyond normal human endurance. Even a natural 20 only grants partial success.

    Vitality

    Vitality measures your capacity to endure damage, exertion, and magical strain. To calculate Vitality, start with a base of 12 and add the modifiers from all three attributes (Corpus, Anima, and Spiritus). Starting Vitality cannot be lower than 1 or higher than 24. Vitality points are both Hit points and fuel for arcane arts. Lost from damage; restored by rest and healing. If reduced to 0, the character suffers Critical Damage. Spending Vitality on arcane feats depletes the same pool.
    Example: Corpus 10 (+0), Anima 6 (−2), Spiritus 16 (+3) 12 + 0 − 2 + 3 = 13 Vitality. Skills, spells, and items can raise this further.
