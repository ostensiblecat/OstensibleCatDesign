Character Rules

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

Careers, jobs, and vocations; whatever name you ascribe to it, you define your approach to the world, not some label or category. Here you’ll find the details to create your character, build your skillset, and find your destiny in the Bleak Forest.

Quick Start

If you have never played a Bleak Forest Engine game before, read this section first. It tells you what you need to know to get started creating your character and where to go to get more details for things like skills, spells, and playing the game.

What You Need

  • One six-sided die (d6) and one twenty-sided die (d20)
  • Pencil and paper (or a printed character sheet)
  • This book

Create Your Character in 4 Steps

Character creation involves determining the statistics, skills, and starting basic equipment for the character.

The character creation process is as follows:

  • Attribute Generation: Roll 3d6 for each stat in order, do not change the scores or alter the order (after all, this is the core conceit of the game). If your starting bonuses total zero or lower, you may roll again.
  • Vitality Calculation: Calculate your starting Vitality score by adding up your attribute bonuses and adding them (or subtracting) the result from a base of 12.
  • Skills and Focus (Option 1): Roll one d6 on each of the three Attribute Skill tables (Corpus, Anima, Spiritus) to determine your starting skill in each area. These three skills are granted free at Apprentice (+1) rank. Next roll for a focus for each skill granted at Competent Rank.
  • Skills and Focus (Option 2): Spend 9 Skill Points on skills and Focus abilities, 1 point per rank. No skill may start above Expert (+3), and no Focus may have more ranks than its skill. See Skills and Focus (Option 2) below.
  • Determine starting gear

Key Terms to Know Now

  • Advantage / Disadvantage: Advantage shifts the BAM! down (easier) and Disadvantage shifts it up (harder), in steps. Four tiers are used throughout: Minor (+1/−1 to the roll), Moderate (1 step), Major (2 steps), Extreme (3 steps). See Advantage and Disadvantage in Playing the Game.
  • Anima: one of the three core attributes. Anima represents your mind and mental capabilities. It reflects your emotions, determination, and how you interact with others and is tied to skills that require charm, finesse, or specialized knowledge.
  • Attribute: one of three core statistic (Corpus, Anima, Spiritus) that help define a character’s physical and mental capabilities.
  • BAM! (Basic Activity Modifier): The target number you must meet or beat on a roll to succeed for checks and saves. A higher BAM! means the task is harder.
  • Condition: A wound or impairment to an attribute. Minor through Extreme severity. Conditions impose penalties until healed.
  • Corpus: one of the three core attributes. Corpus represents your body and physical capabilities. It reflects how well you can lift things, climb, dodge, and how well you endure hardship. Corpus is tied to skills that require physical prowess such as combat, defensive manoeuvring, and survival.
  • Doom: a fate track used by games that track it, such as Hail Three Wyrd Sisters. It starts at 6 (maximum 12); at 0 the character is lost. A Doom save is d20 + Spiritus modifier against the listed BAM!. Games that do not use Doom ignore rules that change it.
  • Save: A defensive roll (d20 + attribute modifier) to resist something bad. Named by the attribute: “Corpus save”, “Anima save”, “Spiritus save”.
  • Spiritus: one of the three core attributes. Spiritus represents your spirit or essence. It reflects your mystical capability, magical aptitude, and how you interact with supernatural beings. It is tied to skills that require magic and esoteric knowledge.
  • Stat (Statistic): A number that describes part of your character. Stats include the three core Attributes (Anima, Corpus, and Spiritus) as well as other values such as Vitality. Collectively, these values are referred to as stats.
  • Vitality: Your hit points. Lost from damage; restored by resting or healing.
  • Watch: A 4-hour block of time. Six watches make a day. You can travel, rest, forage, or do tasks in a watch.

Might

Some creatures, environments, and Challenges possess Attributes above 18 (beyond the ceiling of human potential). These supernatural values are converted into Might, a rank that determines how overwhelmingly a threat bears down on a character.

Might is not simply a bonus added to dice rolls; it is a tier of dominance that reshapes what is possible when facing the creature. Might is divided into three tiers, each with effects that apply to minimum BAM! ratings, Vitality damage bonus, and impacts to saves against the creature’s attacks and abilities.

The benefits of Might tiers ONLY apply to stats that have a might tier assigned – higher level Might benefits do not transfer down to a creature’s non-elevated stats.

Example in action: A Frost Giant has a Corpus score of 24, making its Might of 6. This places it in Tier II (Dread). If a character tries to resist being knocked prone by the Giant's club with a Corpus save, the required BAM! check is at Challenging (19). When the Giant hits, it inflicts its standard 2d6+3 damage plus an additional 6 automatic bonus Vitality damage (based on its Might score of 6) and forces a save against being knocked prone. Should the character roll a natural 20 on their saving throw, they manage to stay upright but still suffer a Minor (Bloodied) condition from the resulting shockwave.

Assigning Might to Environmental Threats Environmental threats usually don’t have stats or a BAM! rating. If they do list a BAM!, use that to pick a Might score. If they don’t, use survivability as your benchmark:

  • Might 1–4: Harsh but survivable conditions Equivalent Difficulty: 16 (Difficult) Examples: severe cold, dangerous terrain, smoke-filled rooms A normal person could survive with effort.
  • Might 5–7: Lethal conditions Equivalent Difficulty: 19 (Challenging) Examples: raging fires, collapsing caverns, toxic atmospheres A normal person would need extraordinary luck or special training to survive.
  • Might 8+: Superhuman-level forces Equivalent Difficulty: 22 (Formidable) Examples: volcanic eruptions, crushing pressure, magical storms Survival is impossible without superhuman abilities.

Quick rule of thumb:

  • Might 1–4: an ordinary person could survive without help
  • Might 5–7: survival requires extraordinary luck or power
  • Might 8+: survival is impossible without superhuman aid

Corpus (Body)

Corpus represents your physical body. It affects how well you can lift things, climb, dodge, and how well you endure hardship. Use your Corpus modifier for melee damage, ranged attacks, and how much you can carry. Checks using Corpus are for things like endurance, moving around, and falling. Your Corpus modifier also increases your Vitality. If your Corpus ever drops to zero, you die.

Anima (Mind)

Anima represents your mind. It reflects your emotions, determination, and how you interact with others. Anima helps with tasks like convincing people, spotting danger, or noticing hidden things. It’s important for resisting effects that target your mind, like charms or illusions. The Anima modifier is added to reaction checks and shows how many loyal followers or magical creatures you can have (if it’s negative, treat it as zero). It also adds to your Vitality. If Anima ever drops to zero, you become unresponsive.

Spiritus (Spirit)

Spiritus represents your inner spirit or essence. It’s important for things like casting spells, empowering sigils, and making deals with magical beings. Your Spiritus modifier is added to spellcasting rolls and to your Vitality. If your Spiritus ever drops to zero, you fall into a coma.

Vitality

Vitality measures your capacity to endure damage, exertion, and magical strain. To calculate Vitality, start with a base of 12 and add the modifiers from all three attributes (Corpus, Anima, and Spiritus). Starting Vitality cannot be lower than 1 or higher than 24.

Example: Corpus 10 (+0), Anima 6 (−2), Spiritus 16 (+3) + 12 + 0 − 2 + 3 = 13 Vitality. Skills, spells, and items can raise this further.

Equipage

Inventory

Your base inventory is 10 slots for items. Your Corpus modifier modifies the number of slots you have available to you. For example:

  • 16 Corpus has a modifier of +3 for a total of 13 slots
  • 4 Corpus has a modifier of -3 for a total of 7 slots

Most items take up one inventory slot, though small and tiny items may be bundled to take up one slot. For instance, a longsword takes up a single slot, while a scroll case may carry up to 10 scrolls and is considered one slot. Inventory capacity has an impact on movement rates and fatigue (See Encumbrance and Fatigue under Conditions, Death, Damage, and Recursion).

Consumables

You have a provisions pouch in which you can keep at the ready food, torches and water. The pouch can store up to SIX of each item (not counted against your inventory total), with any excess being placed into your backpack (and being tracked against your inventory total).

Food, Torches, and Water: Track each consumable with a d6. The number showing is how many uses remain. Lower the die by 1 when you use that supply. When it reaches 0, remove it from your inventory.

  • Food and water: Lower both dice by 1 each time you take a Short Rest or Full Rest; a rest taken without both restores nothing. Each Watch of Forced March costs 1 extra use of each. If either supply runs out, remove its die. After 24 hours without food or water, make a Corpus save; on a failure, add 1 Fatigue marker to your inventory. Make another save each Watch you remain without that supply.
  • Torches: Lower the torch die by 1 for each hour a torch burns. At 0, you have no torches left. Without another light source, such as magic, you cannot see in darkness. In the Bleak Forest, you are At Risk and add 1 to the Danger Die (up to a maximum of 4) while you remain in darkness.

Other consumables such as spell and alchemical components are found via foraging, looting slain enemies, or trade. These consumables are often tiny in size and necessary to perform arcane feats and are often closely guarded. You store these in a components pouch. See Paths arcane for more details.

Item sizes

Tiny (T) items are things like coins, gems, etc. They can be bundled together in a container such as a pouch, bag, etc. 100 tiny items bundled together take up 1 inventory space.

Small (S) items are things like arrows, bolts, etc. Twenty (20) Small items can be bundled together in a container such as a quarrel or quiver taking up 1 inventory space

Regular (R) items are things like longswords, a coil of rope, etc. These take up 1 inventory space

Bulky (B) items take up two slots. These items are usually two-handed or awkward items. While you wear Bulky armour, your Anima modifier is halved (rounded down) for Anima checks and saves only; it does not change your Vitality.

You cannot carry more items than you have inventory slots. If all your slots are full and you take another item into your inventory (fatigue for example), then you are immobilized. Pack animals, hirelings, wagons, etc. can be used to increase capacity (however, fatigue cannot be transferred).

Damage

Weapons have a damage rating that determines how much damage is inflicted on a target. The damage a weapon does is tied to the durability rating a follows:

  • For each point of durability loss on a weapon, the damage the weapon can inflict is reduced by one point
  • A weapon always does at least one point of damage on a successful hit unless it is broken (0 durability rating).

Armour

The Armour Rating is a measure of how much damage armour can deflect or absorb when struck. This value depends on the armour's durability. Every time durability drops by one point; the Armour Rating decreases by one as well. If the armour's durability reaches zero, it breaks and offers no protection.

Here's how damage absorption works:

  • Against simple or improvised weapons: If the attack's damage doesn't exceed the Armour Rating, the blow is fully absorbed and deals no Vitality damage. On a critical hit, the armour still loses 1 Durability.
  • Against martial or ranged weapons: Armour blocks damage up to its rating, but these weapons always inflict at least 1 Vitality damage, as they're meant to pierce or batter through protection.

In all cases, any damage that surpasses the Armour Rating reduces Vitality as normal.

Throw

This indicates whether the weapon may be thrown and used as a missile weapon. Weapons not specifically designed for this task are not balanced and take a penalty when thrown as follows:

  • T0: Can be thrown at no penalty up to Near range (25 m) (Axes and Daggers are balanced for throwing)
  • T1: Can be Thrown at a Minor Disadvantage up to Near Range
  • T2: Can be Thrown at a Major Disadvantage up to Near Range

Durability

The durability rating of weapons and armour is a measure of how much use and damage these items can endure before they become Broken (0 Durability) and require repair. This rating determines the longevity of equipment during combat and other challenging situations. Durability for well maintained, non-magical armours is one point more than the armour rating.

Armour Durability

Armour loses 1 point of durability under the following circumstances:

  • When you suffer a critical hit (rolling a 20 on a d20).
  • When Critical Damage is sustained (damage to an attribute).

Weapon Durability

Weapons lose 1 point of durability in these situations:

  • When you fumble (rolling a 1 on a d20).
  • If your damage roll is completely negated by armour (net damage is 0 after applying armour reduction).

Martial Weapons and Ranged weapons always do at least 1 point of damage due to their qualities as purpose built combat arms. However, if their damage would otherwise be negated by the armour rating, then the durability loss applies. Weapons that successfully deal at least 1 point of damage after armour reduction do not lose durability from the impact itself.

Wear and Tear After Combat

Your Wear Threshold is a measure of how well your gear is maintained and has an impact on the durability of your equipment. At the conclusion of a combat encounter, roll 1d20 and compare the result to your current Wear Threshold. If the roll meets or exceeds the threshold, either your armour or weapons sustain 1 point of durability damage (your choice which). The threshold begins at 20 and decreases by 1 after each encounter without maintenance. When preventative maintenance is carried out, the threshold resets to 20.

Repair and Preventative Maintenance

To repair or maintain arms and armour, the following Abilities, skills, or tradesperson be used:

Skills

Skills represent broad areas of trained ability and expertise. They determine how likely a character is to succeed at various tasks and are associated with one of the three core attributes: Corpus, Anima, or Spiritus. Skills have a Core Skill and associated Focus abilities. The Core Skills are generalist areas that cover the broad spectrum of capabilities, while Focus areas amplify and add new capabilities on top of the abilities granted by a Core skill. You must first possess a Core Skill to be able to gain a Focus ability. Further, you cannot have more ranks in a Focus area than you possess in the Core skill.

Skill Checks

To attempt a skill check, roll a d20 and follow these steps:

  • Apply the relevant BAM! difficulty for the task (Average 13 is the default unless otherwise specified).
  • Apply any Advantage or Disadvantage to adjust BAM!
  • Add the appropriate attribute modifier.
  • Include one point for each rank in the relevant skill.

Skill Progression

Skills use a five-rank system for base proficiency, with a separate three-tier Focus track. The base skill ranks are:

  • Apprentice (+1): you have studied and possess book or trained knowledge if not practical experience
  • Skilled (+2): you have advanced beyond a learner and have practical skill
  • Expert (+3): your skill is evident and you can hold your own in most instances
  • Master (+4): you are extremely practiced in your field and have spent much time in advanced study
  • Grand Master (+5): you have achieved the pinnacle of skill. There are none better

Focus tiers are Competent, Accomplished, and Adept. These provide additional abilities but cannot exceed the base skill rank.

Skill Focus

Skill Focus grants extra benefits to a Core skill. To gain a Skill Focus, you must already possess the Core skill, and your Focus Rank cannot surpass your main skill rank (for example, an Apprentice in Alchemy cannot have a Focus beyond Competent).

  • Competent (Rank 1): beyond training, you have an innate aptitude for the area of focus
  • Accomplished (Rank 2): your skill and aptitude combine to make you capable of impressive feats
  • Adept (Rank 3): you have an innate ability beyond what most are capable of

Skill Focus benefits vary by skill, but they may include:

  • Adjusting BAM! Difficulty Ratings for Activity Checks by adding an Advantage:
  • Competent = Moderate Advantage
  • Accomplished = Major Advantage
  • Adept = Extreme Advantage
  • Gaining incremental advantages with each Focus rank, such as:
  • Extra Action (such as an additional attack or increased movement rate)
  • Additional Companions
  • Extra bonuses for recovery, Vitality, and other aspects

Corpus, Anima, and Spiritus skills

Skills are broken down into skillsets under the three attributes. Within these skillsets, Skill Focus provide you with avenues for specialised expertise in areas such as Athleticism, Skulduggery, and Arcane. Skill Focus Ranks reflect increasing competence within the selected specialized focus area.

When you start the game, you don’t automatically have any weapon skills unless you choose Combat as one of your Corpus skills. You can still try to use weapons, but your ability is based on a BAM! check that uses only your Corpus attribute modifier for the result.

Athleticism

Core: You train your body and mind for top performance and strength. Add your skill rank to physical actions like jumping, climbing, or escaping from danger. You also add your Athleticism rank to Corpus saves against physical effects such as being knocked down, grappled or thrown. This does not stack with Endurance; use the higher rank.

Focus: Chain Reaction

You can perform several different physical actions in one round, like moving, attacking, and retreating. As long as you don’t repeat the same action consecutively, you don’t add a mishap die for Diminishing Returns (See Diminishing Returns under Playing the Game). For each skill focus rank you have, you get the following advantages on these actions:

  • Competent = Moderate Advantage and reduce the Mishap die by maximum of 1
  • Accomplished = Major Advantage and reduce the Mishap dice by maximum of 2
  • Adept = Extreme Advantage and reduce the Mishap dice by maximum of 3

Advancement

The Bleak Forest uses an advancement system called Degrees of the Road. There are nine Degrees. Each Degree represents a meaningful threshold that you have attained. It’s not just about what you can do, but what you have survived and how the world has shaped you.

Degrees are earned through Ordeals – a scenario that as an agent of destiny, you have been called to face. Ordeals are presented through the agents of Fate (in Escape the Bleak Forest and Bleak Forest- Hail Three Wyrd Sisters, the Three Wyrd Sisters act as Fate’s avatars). While not random, Ordeals don’t necessarily occur at fixed points. Certain criteria have to be met, before the agent of Fate appears and an Ordeal arises. Through accumulated Fragments, Omens, and other plot triggers, Ordeals will present themselves once the specified conditions have been met. You advance when the Ordeal has been satisfied and the world confirms that you have changed.

Earning Advancement

You advance to the next Degree when you have met the conditions required. Based upon the particular implementation of the Bleak Forest rules, this can include:

  • Completing at least one Ordeal of the appropriate tier since your last Degree advancement
  • Accumulated the required number of Fragments about the Abrikoth active in your current Region
  • Fulfilled your Drive; or updated it, if it has been completed or has become impossible

Degree advancement happens at the end of a session in which all the conditions were met. When you advance common advantages upon attaining a new degree can include:

  • Gain skill points as listed in the Degree table
  • Gain the Degree ability (unique to that Degree and dependent upon the version of Bleak Forest being used)
  • Doom restores to its current maximum (for BF systems that use Doom)
  • Doom maximum increases by 1 (for BF systems that use Doom)

The Number of Ordeals required is a recommendation only and the specific Bleak Forest Implementations have more defined rules for advancement. Adjust these thresholds to suit your requirements of your style and taste.

Skill Point Spending

Skill points earned on advancement may be spent immediately or held. Each rank costs 1 point, regardless of rank. Focuses are purchased separately at 1 point each. Your focus rank can never exceed your base skill rank:

  • Apprentice → Skilled: 1 point
  • Skilled → Expert: 1 point
  • Expert → Master: 1 point
  • Master → Grand Master: 1 point
  • Focus: Competent; 1 point (requires Apprentice skill rank or higher)
  • Focus: Accomplished; 1 point (requires Skilled skill rank or higher)
  • Focus: Adept; 1 point (requires Expert base or higher)

You may never advance more than one rank in a single skill per Degree advancement, regardless of points available. This applies equally to Focus ranks. A fully maxed skill path (Grand Master rank + Adept focus) costs 8 points total across multiple Degrees. The road teaches slowly.

Challenges and Ordeals

Challenges and Ordeals are the plot drivers that push the game forward. They are specific tasks that must be overcome to bring about the fulfilment of the cycle. As a chosen agent of Fate, you have been set on a path to overcome these obstacles to define yourself, arise to accept your calling, and restore the cycle. Each provides a scenario for you to overcome and are more fully defined in the modular supplements:

  • Challenges drive your character’s emergence and help establish your identity and place in the Bleak Forest. Challenges are on a personal scale and completing them changes you as a character in the form of Scars Knots, and Vows. Challenges form the core driver of the Escape the Bleak Forest Solo Character Funnel Rules, arising at the behest of the Three Wyrd Sisters to help define your character. For more information, see Escape the Bleak Forest.
  • Ordeals are the driving plot elements in the world of Bleak Forest: Hail Three Wyrd Sisters. By completing Ordeals as set forth by the Sisters, you not only advance the plot of the game, but gain greater standing and achievement in the world of the Bleak Forest through the forms of Degrees of the Road. For more information see Bleak Forest: Hail Three Wyrd Sisters.

Challenges and Ordeals are both structured as Minor, Moderate, and Major, though their scale is somewhat different. Challenges are opportunities to drive personal character development and shape you as a character, while Ordeals drive plotlines and advance not only your growth, but the fate of the world. These are presented as a scenario that must be overcome with an overall issue to be resolved. The challenge behind the Ordeal has a BAM! Rating, though the ordeal itself may be comprised of several encounters, situations, and tasks that must be resolved (but they individually must be under the overall BAM! rating for the Ordeal). For more on Challenges and Ordeals, see Playing the Game.

Locks, Scars, and Vows

When a Player Character successfully completes a Challenge, the attribute challenged becomes locked and cannot be changed with further Recursion. Instead, the character undergoes identity progression when they encounter further challenges to a locked attribute, and the Wyrd Sister who presented the challenge offers her marks. The type of mark available depends on which Sister spoke — the Maiden for Minor Challenges, the Matron for Moderate, the Crone for Major — as described under Challenges and Ordeals in Playing the Game.

Vow

A promise, rule, or personal law. Grants a persistent benefit, always paired with a constraint or obligation. Breaking a Vow suspends its benefit until reaffirmed through play. If a Vow’s constraint becomes impossible to uphold after a Recursion — for example, a Vow that required carrying a specific item that was lost — the Vow may be renegotiated with the Wyrd Sisters at the cost of 1d3 Vitality. The Vow is not broken; it is transformed. The new constraint must be of equal or greater weight than the original. Vow Conflict: when two active Vows demand incompatible actions in a single moment (a Vow never to flee versus a Vow to protect a companion who can only be saved by fleeing), you must choose which Vow to honour and which to break. A broken Vow immediately becomes a Scar: remove the Vow’s bonus, apply the Scar’s burden, and gain 1 Echo as the Forest acknowledges what was lost. The broken Vow cannot be renegotiated — it is part of who you are now. Scar slots follow the normal Condition severity structure (Minor, Moderate, Severe, Extreme); treat a broken Vow as a Minor Scar unless the circumstance of breaking was extreme. This is the only way to gain an Echo other than death.

Scar

A permanent wound—physical, mental, or spiritual. Imposes an ongoing penalty or complication. May unlock unique narrative permissions. A Scar cannot be removed, only reinterpreted.

Knot

A learned survival technique. A reusable, limited special action tied to a specific trigger or circumstance. Represents skill born of suffering, not innate talent.

Completing a Challenge always refines who the character is, not just determining how large their numbers are.

Conditions, Damage, Death, and Recursion

Damage

Taking Physical Damage

When you take physical damage:

  • Reduce the damage taken by your Armour Rating.
  • Subtract any remaining damage from your Vitality.

If armour absorbs all the damage, you lose no Vitality. Some abilities and effects may bypass or reduce armour; these exceptions are explained in their own rules.

Reaching 0 Vitality

When your Vitality falls to 0 or lower, you become Impaired and must immediately make a Critical Damage Check:

Corpus save: d20 + Corpus modifier vs. Difficult (16):

  • Success: Your Vitality becomes 0, but you take no attribute damage from that hit.
  • Failure: Apply the excess damage directly to the relevant attribute—usually Corpus for physical attacks.

Excess damage is the amount by which the attack reduced your Vitality below 0.

While your Vitality remains at 0, all further damage goes directly to the relevant attribute. You do not make another Critical Damage Check.

Shields and Absorb the Blow

A shield normally adds to your Armour Rating. You may instead use it to Absorb the Blow, taking damage on the shield:

  • Buckler: absorbs up to 3 damage
  • Shield: absorbs up to 6 damage
  • Tower Shield: absorbs up to 12 damage

If the attack deals more damage than the shield can absorb, the remaining damage is reduced by armour, then applied to Vitality.

If the attack deals no more than the shield’s limit, apply the damage to the shield’s Durability. Damaged shields can be repaired using the normal equipment repair rules. A shield reduced to 0 Durability is destroyed and is beyond repair.

Conditions

After taking attribute damage and being stabilised, you gain a Condition based on the amount of damage taken. Each attribute has one slot for each Condition level (Minor, Moderate, Severe, and Extreme) which is triggered when you have sustained attribute damage past a defined threshold:

  • Minor: 1–2 points of attribute damage
  • Moderate: 3 or more points, up to half the attribute’s maximum score
  • Severe: More than half the attribute’s maximum score, leaving at least 3 points
  • Extreme: The attribute is reduced to 1 or 2 points

Fatigue

You cannot recover Vitality or attribute points if you lack food, water, or rest. After 24 hours without food, water or rest, make a Corpus save; on a failure, gain 1 Fatigue marker. Each marker takes up one inventory slot. If you have no free slots, you must drop an item or become Immobilised until you make room. Repeat the save each Watch you remain without them.

Removing Fatigue
  • An uninterrupted Short Rest of 1 Watch removes 1 Fatigue marker.
  • An uninterrupted Full Rest of 2 Watches, or 8 hours, removes all Fatigue.
Fatigue Ceiling

Your Fatigue Ceiling equals twice your Corpus modifier, with a minimum of 2. Examples:

  • Corpus modifier +2: Fatigue Ceiling 4
  • Corpus modifier 0 or lower: Fatigue Ceiling 2

If your Fatigue exceeds this limit, you become Immobilised. You cannot move or act, except to speak or make saves.

  • Immediately make a Corpus save against Average (13). On a failure, lose 1 Corpus.
  • Repeat this save every hour you remain at or above your Fatigue Ceiling without addressing the cause.

Eating, drinking, and completing a 4-hour Short Rest end the Immobilised condition and stop the hourly saves.

Corpus lost to Fatigue recovers at a rate of 1 point per Full Rest as per normal. If Corpus falls to 0, you die. If Corpus falls to exactly 1, immediately make one Corpus save against Average (13):

  • Success: Remain at 1 Corpus.
  • Failure: Die.

Make this save only once, when you first reach 1 Corpus.

Example: A character with Corpus 14 has a +2 modifier and a Fatigue Ceiling of 4. The fourth Fatigue marker causes no extra effect. When the character would gain a fifth marker, they become Immobilised and make a Corpus save. On a failure, they lose 1 Corpus. They repeat the save every hour until they eat, drink, and rest.

Poisoned

Minor Disadvantage on Corpus checks and saves, plus any effect the poison lists, until it runs its course or is purged.

Restrained or Held

You cannot move or take physical actions, and attacks against you have Moderate Advantage. Repeat the save at the end of each round unless the source says otherwise.

Bleeding

Lose 1 Vitality at the end of each round (each Tick outside combat) until stopped by a Healing power, a herb that stops Bleeding, or 1 Tick of first aid (Wits check, Average 13).

Bound

You cannot move beyond Close range (3 m) of the point or creature that binds you, but you may still attack and cast.

Charmed

You treat the charmer as Friendly (or better, if the effect says so) and will not knowingly harm them. The effect ends if the charmer or their allies harm you.

Cursed

A lasting magical affliction with the effect its source describes. It ends only by the remedy the source names.

Diseased

Suffer the disease’s listed effect at the end of each day until cured. The disease’s rank sets the cure required.

Distracted

Moderate Disadvantage on Anima checks.

Dominated

You follow the controller’s verbal commands as though they were your own intentions. You cannot be made to kill yourself, and you repeat the save each time you are ordered to act against a core belief or a Vow, or to harm an ally.

Exhausted

Minor Disadvantage on all checks and saves, and you cannot run. A Full Rest clears it.

Frightened

You must move away from the source at full speed each round and cannot take offensive actions. Repeat the save at the end of each of your turns. Fear effects use this condition.

Knocked Prone

You are on the ground. Standing costs your Move action. Melee attacks against you have Moderate Advantage; ranged attacks against you have Moderate Disadvantage.

Locked

You cannot cast spells, powers or sigils for 1 full round.

Petrified

You are fully immobile and cannot act. Your Armour Rating is 6 while it lasts.

Sickened

Minor Disadvantage on all checks and saves, and you cannot eat or drink.

Slowed

Your movement is halved, you take only one action per round, and your attacks are at Moderate Disadvantage.

Stunned

You cannot act, and attacks against you have Moderate Advantage.

Full Recursion: Death

Core rule (used by Hail Three Wyrd Sisters; Escape the Bleak Forest uses its own Recursion instead). When a character dies, make a final Doom save against Average (13).

Success

The Sisters return the character to the road with:

  • 1 Vitality
  • 1 fewer Doom
  • All worn items
  • One chosen possession
  • All Skills, Fragments, Identity Marks, and Road Degree

All other physical inventory is lost.

Failure

The character is permanently lost and becomes a Fragment that may provide information to other characters. The player creates a new character at one Road Degree lower, with the appropriate Skills and Identity Marks.

Echoes

Death leaves traces. Each time a Player Character dies and Recurses, they gain 1 Echo. When death occurs mid-session, all Echoes are banked and resolved at the start of the next session — see Echo Bank below. You may hold a maximum of 3 Echoes at any one time. Echoes persist through Recursion. Echoes you held before dying are retained and count toward the cap of 3 — you then gain 1 new Echo from the death, provided you are below the maximum. However, Echoes may only be spent during your current life or in the first Watch of a new life after Recursion. Any Echo that has survived two consecutive Recursions without being spent is lost — the Forest does not allow grief to accumulate indefinitely.

In Escape the Bleak Forest, spend Echoes as follows:

  • 1 Echo: Keep one rolled starting skill result
  • 1 Echo: Retain one item from starting gear or loot
  • 2 Echoes: Mark one future Challenge attempt as Learned (ignore Mishap dice for that attempt)
  • 3 Echoes: Transform one Scar into a Vow

Echoes ensure that even failure builds meaning — but only if you act on what you have learned.

Echo Bank

Echoes

Each death before Recursion grants 1 Echo. Echoes are banked; up to 3 at a time; and may be spent in the first Watch after returning. Under the core death rule (Hail Three Wyrd Sisters), spend them as follows:

  • Spend 1 Echo: Retain one specific item that was lost on death.
  • Spend 1 Echo: Restore 1d6 Vitality beyond the 1 Vitality received on return.
  • Spend 1 Echo: Carry forward knowledge of one thing the character witnessed at the moment of death; a Fragment, a Herald's location, a Seal Condition. Counts as a discovered Fragment.
  • Spend 2 Echoes: Retain one Identity Mark (Vow, Scar, or Knot) that would otherwise have been lost.

When a character dies mid-session, the mechanical consequences of Recursion are resolved at the start of the next session, not immediately. Death is narrated at the table — describe how the character falls, what the Forest takes, what the last sensation is — but the dice are set aside. The reroll of attributes, the spending of Echoes, and the declaration of starting equipment all happen as the opening act of the next session.

All Echoes held at death are banked and available at the start of that next session. They may be spent freely during the first Watch of the new life. This ensures that Recursion never collapses a session into a reroll sequence — every session ends with meaningful play, and every session begins with a clean, intentional opening.

Exception — If death occurs in the final minutes of a session and both player and narrator prefer to resolve it immediately, they may. The Echo Bank is a pacing tool, not a requirement.
