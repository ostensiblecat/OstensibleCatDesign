Core Rules: Ways to Play

Created by Johnathan L Bingham

Ways to Play

The full rules can seem daunting when taken as a whole. They are written so that one book can accomodate three different modes of play: a party with a Narrator, a lone player with no Narrator at all, and everything between (Co-Op). Not every table needs every procedure. This page gathers the ways to trim the game to the table you have. None of these modifications change the core game mechanics; they only streamline play.

OptionForWhat it does
Lean PlayAny tableSwitches off the subsystems that nothing else depends on.
Guided PlayA Narrator-led partyReplaces the game’s oracles with the Narrator’s judgment, but keeps the feel of the game intact.
Solo PlayOne player, no NarratorKeeps every oracle: they are the Narrator.
Co-Op PlaySeveral players, no NarratorKeeps every oracle, and shares the Narrator’s jobs around the table.

Lean Play combines with either of the others.

Lean Play

The full rules give the Narrator a good deal to track. For a lighter game, you can switch off any of the subsystems below. Nothing else depends on them, so the rest of the game works exactly as written. Choose before play begins, and tell the players which are off.

Switch off Play it this way instead
Weather Make no weather rolls. Terrain alone sets travel speed and the Danger Die.
Item durability Weapons and armour never lose Durability. An item breaks only when the story calls for it, and the Narrator says so.
Mishap dice for extra actions Take one action per turn, with no pushing for more (unless a Skill or Focus allows for it). No Mishap dice build up. A natural 1 still rolls on the Skill Mishap table.
The Corruption pool Apply a Magical Misfire’s immediate result only. It adds nothing lasting.
Named components Use Components by Availability: track only Common, Uncommon and Rare.
Red herrings in Minor Ordeals Seed no False Fragments in Minor Ordeals. Moderate and Major Ordeals keep theirs. In the Ordeal Generator, ignore any False Fragment it seeds in a Minor Ordeal.
Always keep the core roll and Advantage, Vitality and Conditions, the Danger Die, Fragments and Omens, Doom and Boon, and the Fated Road. They are the game itself; lean play only trims what is not necessary for Narrator-guided play.

Guided Play

Many Bleak Forest subsystems are oracles: they answer questions that a solo player or unprepared Narrator would otherwise have to answer themselves. A Narrator at the table can replace any of these subsystems and oracles with their own judgment and lose nothing. The test for any subsystem is simple: If a subsystem works behind the scenes and is not player-facing, the Narrator may decide it. If the players watch it unfold, keep it: it is part of the feel.

Let the Narrator decide

SubsystemIn guided play
Omen rollsAn event must still be Prepared. Then the Omen comes when the story is ready for it, not when a d6 says so.
Weather rollsDescribe the weather when it matters and assign any effects as desired.
The Setback table and the Wyrd die’s Special EventsChoose the Setback or Special Event that fits the scene. A Setback still lowers the Ruin die.
Reaction and disposition rollsPlay creatures as you see them. Keep the reaction roll only if you want to be surprised yourself.
Fragment forms and encounter tablesUse them, or the Ordeal Generator, to prepare between sessions rather than at the table.
Party-size scalingJudge it by eye.
The travel budget and terrain multipliersNarrate travel in Watches and days.

Simplify

  • Danger Die: roll only once per Watch of travel, not on entering every sub-hex. Roll it in the open: a growing pool of dice is part of the dread.
  • Hex scales: use the Overland map and Watches. Zoom in only for a site that matters.
  • Inventory: keep the slots, drop the load bands. Full is full.
  • Fatigue: only for forced marches, hunger and nights without rest.
  • Red herrings: write False Fragments on purpose rather than seeding them by the rules. Let the players reason them out instead of rolling to test them, but keep Insight as their reward.
  • Lean Play: its five switch-offs suit guided play well.

Keep: this is the essence of the Bleak Forest

  • The core roll and Advantage/Disadvantage, including Advantage from completed tasks.
  • Doom and Boon, and above all accepting trouble. The Narrator offers the Adversaries lie or a character’s Complication; the player decides whether to take the Boon. That exchange is where the dread and the players’ agency meet.
  • The Fated Road’s shape: the Goal, Ordeals, Fragments, Threads, Holds, the Heart and the Reckoning. Players feel it through what they find and what they lose.
  • The Ruin die, keep it on the table where everyone can see it. A visible countdown does more than any description to build a sense of impending doom.
  • The decisive check and the near miss. Everything in an Ordeal leads to one roll.
  • Conditions, not Vitality alone adds a cost with lasting impact. Being Maimed or Traumatised is what makes the Forest bite.
  • Drive and Complication: each character’s handles for the Narrator to play off of to add dramatic elements for narrative effect.

Solo players should keep the oracles among these (Omen rolls, Special Events, the Setback table, weather, demeanour, and the Forage and Loot tables): with no Narrator, the tables make the decisions.

Solo Play

A lone player keeps every oracle, because together they are the Narrator: Omen rolls, the Setback table, the Wyrd die, weather, reaction rolls, and the Fragment and encounter tables. The rules for one character are already built in:

  • Party Size: fewer encounters and enemies, and the character Leads every task.
  • Companions as Seconds: Devoted Followers and Soul Bound animals support tasks in place of other characters.
  • Accepting trouble: a solo player takes it from Setbacks and Wyrd die results.
  • The Ordeal Generator and Die Roller run the procedures, and the generator can hide what the Fragments have not yet revealed.
  • The Oracles answer every question no other rule covers: a yes or no oracle, meaning tables, people and Rumours.
  • Keeping the Road Secret shows how to read the Goal tables without spoiling them, and A Solo Session shows how a session runs.

Lean Play works for solo play too.

Co-Op Play

Co-Op Play is two or more players and no Narrator. Everything in Solo Play applies: the oracles run the world. The players share the Narrator’s jobs as follows.

  • The Keeper of the Road. One player holds the Goal tables and the Ordeal Generator, and reads out only what has been revealed (see Keeping the Road Secret). Pass the role to the next player at the start of each new Ordeal, so no one carries the secrets for long. The Keeper still plays their character, but never uses what they have read and not yet revealed.
  • Rolling the oracles. Whoever’s character is least involved in the scene rolls the oracle and the encounter tables, and describes what they show. That player also plays the people the characters meet in that scene.
  • Setting the likelihood. The player who rolls the yes or no oracle sets how likely a yes is. Anyone may object once; if the table disagrees, take the more likely of the two.
  • Deciding as a party. Talk it through, then the characters whose lives it risks decide. If the table is split, the Lead of the current task decides; outside a task, roll the oracle.
  • Accepting trouble. As in Solo Play, trouble comes from Setbacks and Wyrd die results. Any player may also offer trouble to another player’s character from that character’s Complication; the player decides whether to take the Boon (see Accepting trouble).
  • Sharing the spotlight. Go round the table when choosing Watch actions, and give each character a task they Lead in every Ordeal where you can (see Party Size).
  • Safety still matters. Agree on lines, veils and a pause signal before play, just as with a Narrator (see Session Zero and Safety).
Example: Three players start a Moderate Ordeal, and Sam becomes its Keeper. When the party questions a toll-keeper, Ana’s character is keeping watch outside, so Ana rolls his disposition, plays him, and rolls the yes or no oracle when the party asks whether he is lying. When the party finds the Ordeal’s second Fragment, Sam reads out that Fragment and nothing more, and notes it with a question mark, since it may be false.
