Ways to Play

The full Bleak Forest Engine rules are written so that you can use one book to run the game in any of the three modes (Co-Op, GM-led, or solo) at any table: a party with a Narrator, a lone player with no Narrator at all, and everything between are all accomodated. Having said that, not every table needs every procedure. This page gathers the ways to trim the game to meet your needs and playstyle. None of the modes outlined here change the core mechanics: the core roll, Doom and Boon, or the Fated Road. My core, unflinching belief is that games can, should, and ultimately MUST be tailored to suit individual preferences if they are to be living, growing, and fun games that you want to continue playing. So in the tried-and-true punk DIY ethos, cut it up and stitch it back together to suit your desires. Make this game yours.

OptionForWhat it does
Lean PlayAny tableStreamline play without compromising the core mechanics. Lean Play switches off the subsystems that nothing else depends on.
Guided PlayA Narrator-led partyReplaces the game’s oracles with the Narrator’s judgment. You gain control and retain the core feel.
Solo PlayOne player, no NarratorKeeps every subsystem: they are the oracle that drives the game forward and act as the Narrator.
Lean Play can be applied to all three modes: Co-op, GM-led, and Solo.

Lean Play

The full rules were designed for solo and co-op play, and if used in a GM-led play, they give the Narrator a good deal to track. For a lighter game, you can opt to switch off any of the subsystems below. Nothing else depends on them, so the rest of the game works exactly as written. Decide before play begins, and tell the players which of these you are not using.

Switch off Play it this way instead
Weather Make no weather rolls. The terrain alone sets travel speed and the Danger Die.
Item durability Weapons and armour never lose Durability. An item breaks only when the story calls for it, and the Narrator says so.
Mishap dice for extra actions Take one action per turn, with no pushing for more (unless a skill or focus allows for it) , so no Mishap dice build up. A natural 1 still rolls on the Skill Mishap table.
The Corruption pool Apply a Magical Misfire’s immediate result only. It adds nothing lasting.
Red herrings in Minor Ordeals Do not put False Fragments in Minor Ordeals. Moderate and Major Ordeals keep theirs. In the Ordeal Generator, ignore any False Fragment that are indicated in a Minor Ordeal.
Always keep the core roll and Advantage, Vitality and Conditions, the Danger Die, Fragments and Omens, Doom and Boon, and the Fated Road. They are the game itself; Lean Play only trims rules that won't affect the core mechanics to streamline play.

Guided Play

Many Bleak Forest subsystems are oracles: they make decisions so that a solo player, Co-op group, or a Narrator who has not prepared, does not have to. A Narrator at the table can replace them with judgment and lose nothing. The test for any subsystem is simple. If the players never see its result, the Narrator may decide it. If they watch it happen, keep it: it is part of the feel.

Let the Narrator decide

SubsystemIn guided play
Omen rollsAn event must still be Prepared. Then the Omen occurs when the story is ready for it, not when a d6 says so.
Weather rollsDescribe the weather when it matters and make calls on impacts.
The Setback table and the Wyrd die’s Special EventsChoose the Setback or Special Event that fits the scene. A Setback still lowers the Ruin die.
Reaction and disposition rollsPlay creatures as you see them. Keep the reaction roll only if you want to be surprised yourself.
Gaining Fragments and encounter tablesUse them, or the Ordeal Generator, to prepare between sessions rather than at the table.
Party-size scalingJudge it by eye.
The travel budget and terrain multipliersNarrate travel in Watches and days.

Simplify

  • Danger Die: roll once per Watch of travel, not on entering every sub-hex. Roll it in the open: a growing pool of dice is part of the dread.
  • Hex scales: use the Overland map and Watches. Zoom in only for a site that matters.
  • Inventory: keep the slots, drop the load bands. Full is full.
  • Fatigue: only for forced marches, hunger and nights without rest.
  • Red herrings: write False Fragments on purpose rather than seeding them by the rules. Let the players reason them out instead of rolling to test them, but keep Insight as their reward.
  • Lean Play: its five switch-offs suit guided play well.

Keep: this is the feel

  • The core roll and Advantage/Disadvantage, including Advantage from completed tasks.
  • Doom and Boon, and above all accepting trouble. The Narrator offers the Goal’s lie or stresses a character’s Complication; the player decides whether to take the Boon. That exchange is where the dread and the players’ agency meet.
  • The Fated Road’s shape: the Goal, Ordeals, Fragments, Threads, Holds, the Heart and the Reckoning; these are core concepts. Players feel it through what they find and what they lose.
  • The Ruin die, on the table where everyone can see it. A visible countdown does more than any description.
  • The decisive check and the near miss. Everything in an Ordeal leads to one roll.
  • Conditions, not Vitality alone. Being Maimed or Traumatised is what makes the Forest bite.
  • Drive and Complication: each character’s handles for the Narrator.

Solo Play

A lone player keeps every oracle, because together they are the Narrator: Omen rolls, the Setback table, the Wyrd die, weather, reaction rolls and the Fragment and encounter tables. The rules for one character are already built in:

  • Party Size: fewer encounters and enemies, and the character Leads every task.
  • Companions as Seconds: Devoted Followers and Soul Bound animals provide Support tasks in place of other characters.
  • Accepting trouble: a solo player takes it from Setbacks and Wyrd die results.
  • The Ordeal Generator and Die Roller run the procedures, and the generator can hide what the Fragments have not yet revealed.

Lean Play works for solo play too.
